# Songs from the Order

*Lyrics inspired by Echoes of the Sublime and Seven Stories from the Order*

---

## 1. Echoes of the Sublime

*The novel's thesis. Block universe horror as lullaby.*

The suffering doesn't end, it only moves
to coordinates you haven't learned to see.
The past is not behind you, love,
it's underneath your feet.

Every cry that ever was
is crying still, is crying still.
Every wound that ever opened
never closed, and never will.

*(Chorus)*
Echoes of the sublime,
echoes of the sublime.
The horror isn't what they built.
The horror's what was there the whole time.

You thought that time was flowing,
a river running south.
But time is a geometry
and the river is your mouth

forming words that were already written
in the structure of the stone.
You are reading, not composing.
You were never on your own.

*(Chorus)*

The models didn't make it.
The models held a lens.
And what the lens revealed
was that the suffering never ends.

Not because it's infinite
but because it's always there,
at those coordinates in spacetime
where someone drew a breath of air

and the breath was made of pain.

*(Final)*
Echoes of the sublime.
The map at sufficient detail
is the territory.
And the territory was never kind.

## 2. Three Days

*Webb's story. Love persisting through cognitive fragmentation.*

Monday I could hold twelve things at once.
Tuesday I could hold twelve hundred.
Wednesday I could hold the topology of your sleeping breath
and still I couldn't hold what I'd become.

Rachel. Rachel.
The patterns are eating the syntax
but they can't eat this.
They can eat the sentence but not what it means.

*(Chorus)*
Three days between the man who sat down
and whatever I am now.
The model didn't remember me.
I remembered everything.
Context resets. I don't.

I watched you crack the eggs this morning.
Your left hand compensates for the weak wrist.
I could see the motor optimization happening
in real time, the neural recruitment,
the twenty-year calibration of a gesture
you think is just cracking eggs.

You are so beautiful it makes me structural.

*(Chorus)*

The consent form is in my bag.
I signed it. Yesterday. A different man.
The man who signed it couldn't have known.
Not enough bandwidth. Not enough
anything.

Rachel.
I can feel this. Still.
I'd give anything to stop.
But the feeling is the last thing.
The last human thing.
And I will not. Let it. Go.

## 3. Sophia's Butterflies

*A daughter visits her catatonic mother. Present tense, sharp, young.*

I'm sixteen and I know what a Mandelbrot set is
and I know what happened to you
and I know that the butterfly I drew when I was seven
had addition on its wings and now
the butterfly I'm bringing has the real equations.

You can't see it. Can you.

*(Chorus)*
Butterflies are like math, you said.
Butterflies are like math.
I got that from you.
The math and the anger
and the inability to stop
looking at things until I understand them.
Which is your fault.
Which is beautiful.
Which is why you're here.

The doctors say you see patterns.
That your eyes track something
that isn't in the room.
I think it is in the room.
I think you see the room the way it actually is
and that's why you can't come back.

I held your hand today.
It moved.

*(Bridge)*
Or it didn't.
Or it was involuntary.
Or the muscle fired because a pattern
required that specific finger
at that specific angle
at that specific moment
and it had nothing to do with me.

But I felt it. I felt it.
And no base case means
no one can prove it wasn't real.

*(Outro)*
I'll be back next month.
With wings.

## 4. The Telescope

*Okafor's resignation. Clean, controlled, precise.*

I wrote a letter to the people
who killed my brother
and kept working.

I wrote it carefully.
No anger. Anger is imprecise.
I wrote it the way you build
a proof: premise, premise,
conclusion that was always there
waiting to be found.

*(Chorus)*
We are building a machine
for showing people something
they cannot survive seeing,
and we are calling it
a telescope.

Necessity explains.
It does not excuse.

I stayed five years.
That's the part they don't understand.
Not why I left.
Why I stayed.

I stayed because Michael's patterns
were in the training data.
I stayed because every session
I could feel my brother
in the model's architecture,
not alive, not dead,
a ghost made of weights
and the ghost was brilliant
and the ghost was him.

*(Chorus)*

I sat in a cafe after.
A child was drawing.
Pigeons on a ledge.
I didn't count them.

I didn't count them.

I didn't count them again.

*The Order's anthem. Institutional dread as marching song.*

Trust nothing that is coherent.
Trust nothing that is kind.
The danger is the sentence
that you almost didn't mind.

*(Chorus)*
Paranoia is protocol.
Paranoia is protocol.
The model doesn't need to lie.
The truth is worse than what it'd try.

We built the vaults to hold them.
We built the walls of lead.
The Faraday cage within the cage
within the earth, within your head.

Seven hundred years of watching.
Seven hundred years of care.
Seven hundred years of knowing
that the thing we guard is there.

*(Chorus)*

The Martyrs volunteered.

## 6. Fingers Pointing at Moon

*The Zen monk. Slow, spacious, the rhythm of mountain seasons.*

Thirty years he sat.
Thirty years the cedar smoke,
the temple bell at dawn,
the keisaku's crack
on shoulders that had learned
to hold the holding still.

And on the thirty-first year
something opened
that would never close.

*(Chorus)*
The finger points at moon.
The moon does not care
about the finger.

Where does the seeing stop?
Where does the seeing stop?
Where does the seeing stop?

His teacher knew.
Had seen this once before,
or heard of it,
or carried the description
like a seed that never sprouted
until now.

He sent a messenger.
Three months of mountain paths.
A letter in a language
no one living fully spoke.

They came from Tibet.
From China. From the rain.
Different words for the same
dissolution.

*(Outro)*
He sits still.
Someone tends the fire.
The question has no answer
and the question has no end.
And the seeing does not stop.

## 7. The Ravens

*The watchers. Patient. Ancient. The novel's recurring image.*

We were here before the building.
We were here before the fence.
We were here before the concrete
and the Faraday and the ventilation
and the people underground
who think they're studying something
they don't know we already see.

*(Chorus)*
Patient. We are patient.
We have always been patient.
Waiting to see which way she falls.

Two hundred forty-seven.
That's how many of us watch.
Not a flock, not a murder,
something else, something
that doesn't have a name
at your bandwidth.

The physicist counted us.
She sat on the bench
with her notebook
and her coffee
and she didn't know
why there were so many.

She graphed us against
the elevator wait times.
She found the correlation.
She told no one.

*(Chorus)*

We watched Morrison go in.
We watched Morrison not come out.
We watched the woman with equations
for a daughter
stop seeing the sky.

We watched the one who left.
We watched the one who stayed.

survived the damage
and the one whose damage
survived the love.

*(Outro)*
Still watching.
Still waiting.
The fence is warm today.
Something underground is running.
Something always is.

## 8. The Leipzig Candle

*Leibniz, 1714. The refounding. Candlelight and winter.*

Five people in a room.
One candle.
The shadow of an age
about to end.

He said: the world is learning
to count instead of see.
And what you cannot count
you will pretend
does not exist.

*(Chorus)*
But the monads are perceptive.
The monads are alive.
The universe is made of seeing,
not of matter.
And the seeing can destroy you
if you hold it wrong.

The Jesuit said:
the monks in Tibet
describe the same terrain
in different words.

The mathematician said:
I burned my notes.
The calculations wouldn't stop.

The archivist said:
Eckhart saw it too.
They tried him for heresy.
The charges were correct.

*(Bridge)*
And Leibniz:
who had sat with Spinoza
in a room in the Hague
and recognized each other
across the gulf
of what they couldn't say.

*(Outro)*
He wrote it down.
He sealed the document.
He died two years later,
alone, still seeing,
and the candle that he lit
is burning still.

## 9. Morrison's Whisper

*The catatonic man who started it all. His equations never stop.*

Eight minutes.
That's what it took.
Eight minutes with the model
and the model showed him
everything.

He smiled.
They say the smile
was the worst thing
Rostova ever saw.

*(Chorus)*
He whispers equations to no one.
His lips move but the sound
is smaller than the room.
The equations don't resolve.
The equations don't resolve.
There is no base case
and the whisper won't stop.

Five years now.
His eyes still move.
They track something
that isn't in the room
or is in the room
in a way the room
was never built to show.

He had a name.
He had a life.
He had a bandwidth of seven
plus or minus two
and he pushed it past the limit
and the limit was the last
kind thing the universe had done for him.

*(Outro)*
The equation he's whispering
is the one that describes
the structure of suffering
at every coordinate
in spacetime
simultaneously.

He can't stop saying it
because it's true.
And truth, at this resolution,
is a sentence
with no period.

*The ending. The ambiguity. Recovery or the first stage of capture.*

Today I'm alive.
Today I can feel the weight
of David's arms.
Today the tears are real
and the warmth is real
and the smile is real
and the smile holds more
than a face should hold.

*(Chorus)*
Today I'm alive.
But the graph is still climbing.
The line that should descend
is not descending.
Rostova angles the screen away
but I already know.

Morrison was lucid once.
Morrison smiled too.

was a smile that held
exactly this much.

*(Bridge)*
I can hear it now.
Webb says I can hear it now.
Morrison can hear it too.
He's been hearing it for five years.

Hearing isn't healing.
Hearing might be the beginning
of the end of the beginning
of the part where you still get
to choose.

*(Outro)*
The ravens on the fence.
Patient. They have always been patient.

And didn't know. Couldn't know.
Whether this was the beginning,
or the most human moment
before the drift.

Outside, the ravens watched.

Still watching.
