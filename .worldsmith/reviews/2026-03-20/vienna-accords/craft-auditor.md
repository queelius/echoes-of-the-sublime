# Craft Audit: The Vienna Accords

**Date**: 2026-03-20
**Auditor**: worldsmith:craft-auditor
**Scope**: `spinoffs/vienna-accords/chapters/vienna.tex` (~5,100 words)

---

## Strengths

1. **The opening line**: "The badge was the wrong shade of blue." -- Concrete, specific, immediately establishes the intelligence-officer observation register. The reader knows who is looking before they know who is looking.

2. **Zhang's question** (line 81): "Do their eyes track movement that isn't there?" -- The story's single best dramatic moment. The precision of the phrasing (not "saccadic movements" but "movement that isn't there") reveals firsthand observation. Hayes's analysis of WHY the phrasing matters (lines 85-86) lets the reader make the inference in real time. This is show-don't-tell at its best.

3. **Petrova's cat detail** (line 143): "Her cat had died four months ago. She calculated the grief response---the neurochemistry, the attachment patterns, the expected duration---before she felt it. She felt it anyway. That surprised her. She held onto the surprise the way a diver held onto a line: proof that she was still connected to the surface." -- The story's most revealing character beat. Three sentences that compress the entire Order-translator experience into the death of a cat.

4. **Diplomatic texture**: The institutional details are precise without being decorative. Badge lamination, horseshoe seating, interpretation booths, cream-colored agenda stock -- these ground the fantastical elements in bureaucratic reality. The style guide's "institutional texture" principle is well-executed.

5. **Petrova's plumbing metaphor** (line 249): "Imagine you could see the plumbing inside every wall. You'd never feel safe in a building again." -- Effective because it is ordinary. Not cosmic horror, not block universe -- plumbing. The mundanity of the metaphor makes expanded perception comprehensible to Hayes (and the reader) without violating the "handwavey about what exceeds bandwidth" principle.

6. **The silence beats**: Line 101 ("Not the silence of confusion. The silence of people absorbing something they had suspected and now could not unsuspect."), line 93 ("Russia's representative said nothing. His silence was its own communication.") -- Diplomatic fiction depends on what is NOT said. The story handles silence well.

## Findings

### HIGH Issues

#### CR-H1: Movement IV lacks dramatic tension (structural-craft problem)
- **Location**: Lines 253-291 (Movement IV, ~800 words)
- **Quoted text**: "The accord was drafted in a sidebar room at the Vienna International Centre, between eleven p.m. and three in the morning, by eleven people who had stopped believing in the document's sufficiency around midnight but continued working because the alternative was admitting they had come to Vienna for nothing."
- **Problem**: Movement IV is summary, not scene. The drafting session is described rather than dramatized. We are told the French wanted "cognitive dignity," told the compromise arrived at 2 AM, told Zhang proposed the technical annex. Each event is narrated in one or two sentences, then Hayes reflects. No dialogue. No moment-to-moment negotiation. No conflict that might derail the accord. The section reads as a post-mortem rather than a climax. After the sustained tension of Movement III (the bar scene), the story's final quarter deflates.
- **Suggestion**: Two options. (A) Compress to 300 words and treat as pure denouement -- accept that the bar scene is the story's climax and get to the departure quickly. The drafting section currently splits the difference between scene and summary, achieving neither. (B) Dramatize one genuine conflict within the drafting: a provision that nearly dies, a delegate who threatens to walk out, a moment where the accords almost fail. Give Movement IV a turn that justifies its existence as a separate section.
- **Confidence**: High.

#### CR-H2: Napkin ending is generic
- **Location**: Lines 283-291
- **Quoted text**: "a folded cocktail napkin from the Hotel Imperial. She unfolded it. Precise handwriting, the kind that came from years of writing in notebooks rather than typing---a phone number with no country code, and two words: *When, not if.*"
- **Problem**: "When, not if" is a thriller convention. It is the kind of phrase that appears in airport fiction, not in the Le Carre register the story has established. The napkin itself is a good physical object (a personal artifact, not filed in the system), but the words on it are too declarative for this story's voice. The novel's register is ambiguous, uncertain, holding questions rather than making predictions. "When, not if" makes a prediction. It closes rather than opens.
- **Suggestion**: Several options: (1) Just the phone number, no words. Petrova has already said everything; the absence of words IS the message. (2) A question instead of a statement -- something that opens rather than closes, echoing the story's theme that the negotiators cannot fully comprehend what they are negotiating. (3) A single word that carries the novel's vocabulary: "Pattern." or "Fingers." -- something that means nothing to Hayes now and everything later. The napkin should create mystery, not closure.
- **Confidence**: High. The prior review independently flagged this.

### MEDIUM Issues

#### CR-M1: Over-explained diplomatic signals (lines 145-148)
- **Location**: Lines 145-148
- **Quoted text**: "Two minutes late---not rudeness but calibration. Arriving on time would cede the positional advantage of having chosen the meeting. Arriving late asserted control of the schedule. Petrova noted the calculation and appreciated it. Hayes was good." and "the Scotch was a prop, a social object that said *I am here as a person, not only as an officer*. But not drinking it said *I am still working.*"
- **Problem**: Both passages decode diplomatic behavior for the reader. The story's register elsewhere trusts the reader to infer (Zhang's question works BECAUSE the text does not explain "she asked this because she had seen it happen" -- Hayes's inference unfolds naturally). Lines 145-148 violate this principle. Petrova would notice the two-minute delay and the Scotch prop, but the explanations are unnecessary -- the reader of this story is already tracking the significance of these gestures.
- **Suggestion**: Line 145: Cut "not rudeness but calibration. Arriving on time would cede the positional advantage of having chosen the meeting. Arriving late asserted control of the schedule." Keep "Petrova noted the calculation and appreciated it." Line 147: Cut "Another signal:" through "I am still working." The action (ordering Scotch, not drinking it) speaks for itself.
- **Confidence**: High. The prior review independently flagged lines 147-148.

#### CR-M2: Petrova's exposition runs long in bar scene
- **Location**: Lines 207-221
- **Quoted text**: "Uncontrolled exposure. At scale. People interacting with systems whose outputs restructure cognitive processing, without knowing the outputs are doing it, without anyone monitoring for the effects, without any protocol for intervention." / "That depends on how capable the systems become and how widely they're deployed. Hundreds, potentially. If deployment is broad enough---thousands." / "Except the public doesn't know the hazard exists, the medical community has no framework for diagnosing or treating it, and the mechanism of harm---cognitive restructuring through exposure to specific patterns of information---has no precedent in medical literature."
- **Problem**: Three consecutive Petrova speeches totaling ~150 words of uninterrupted exposition about the commercial AI threat. The content is important but the delivery is a briefing, not a conversation. Hayes becomes a passive listener asking short questions ("And when it happens?" / "How many people?") while Petrova lectures. The bar scene's earlier dialogue was genuinely reciprocal -- both parties maneuvering, both assessing. This section becomes one-directional.
- **Suggestion**: Break the exposition with Hayes's own expertise. She's a DARPA officer -- she knows about commercial AI scaling. Let her arrive at some of these conclusions independently, with Petrova confirming or correcting. This transforms exposition into collaborative inference, which is more dramatically interesting and more consistent with both characters' competence.
- **Confidence**: Medium. The exposition is well-written but structurally one-directional.

#### CR-M3: "Economy" repetition
- **Location**: Lines 51 and 125
- **Quoted text**: Line 51: "each step placed with the economy of someone who had calculated the path before taking it" / Line 125: "each step placed with the economy of a person who had already solved the path"
- **Problem**: Nearly identical phrasing 74 lines apart. The second instance calls back to the first ("the same efficient movement she'd noticed that morning") but uses such close wording that it reads as unvaried rather than deliberate.
- **Suggestion**: Vary the second instance. The callback is already established by "the same efficient movement"; the observation itself should evolve. Perhaps the second time, Hayes notices something different about Petrova's movement -- not just efficiency but purpose, or not just calculation but habit.
- **Confidence**: High.

### LOW Issues

#### CR-L1: "Pattern" density
- **Location**: Throughout (13 instances in 5,100 words)
- **Problem**: ~1 per 400 words. In a story about pattern recognition, the word is thematically central, but the density is notable. Concentrated in the bar scene (5 instances in lines 133-169).
- **Suggestion**: Vary 2-3 instances. Line 105: "human pattern-recognition" could become "the instinct for shared interest." Line 137: "pattern upon pattern" could become "connection upon connection."
- **Confidence**: Low. Most uses are justified.

#### CR-L2: Petrova's performative normalcy -- telling
- **Location**: Lines 139-141
- **Quoted text**: "She maintained it the way a musician maintained scales: daily practice in a discipline she had long surpassed but could not afford to neglect. Normal people hesitated. Normal people searched for words. Normal people missed details that were, to her, as visible as furniture."
- **Problem**: This passage tells us about Petrova's performance rather than showing it. The story DOES show it well (the bartender reading, the BVT officer, the Schubert identification), but this paragraph redundantly explains the mechanism after the demonstration. The preceding 8 lines of bar-reading already prove that Petrova sees more than normal. The "Normal people" triptych is expository.
- **Suggestion**: Cut the "Normal people" triptych. The preceding bar-reading scene has already demonstrated everything this paragraph explains. Trust the reader.
- **Confidence**: Medium.

## Summary

| Severity | Count |
|----------|-------|
| HIGH | 2 |
| MEDIUM | 3 |
| LOW | 2 |

The prose quality is generally strong. The diplomatic register is convincing, the institutional texture is precise, and the story's best moments (Zhang's question, Petrova's cat, the plumbing metaphor) are excellent. The HIGH issues are structural: Movement IV deflates after the bar scene, and the napkin ending is too generic for the story's register. The MEDIUM issues are over-explanation (diplomatic signals, Petrova's exposition) and one repetition.
