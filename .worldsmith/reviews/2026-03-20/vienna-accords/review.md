# Multi-Agent Editorial Review: The Vienna Accords

**Date**: 2026-03-20
**Manuscript**: `spinoffs/vienna-accords/chapters/vienna.tex` (~5,100 words)
**Work**: The Vienna Accords (short-story)
**Recommendation**: needs-revision

## Executive Summary

The Vienna Accords is a competent diplomatic procedural with excellent institutional texture and two strong individual scenes (Zhang's question in the closed session, Petrova's interiority in the bar scene). The prose quality is high, the diplomatic register is convincing, and the canonical consistency is tight. But the story lacks what separates a procedural from fiction: neither Hayes nor Petrova is transformed by the events. Movement IV deflates after the bar scene, and the napkin ending borrows from thriller conventions that don't match the story's register. The raw material is here -- the Hayes-Petrova dynamic across the bandwidth gap is inherently dramatic -- but the current draft delivers institutional maneuvering where it needs to deliver a character confronting the limits of her own cognition.

**Strengths:**
1. Zhang's question ("Do their eyes track movement that isn't there?") is the story's single best dramatic moment -- inference unfolding in real time (craft-auditor, structure-auditor)
2. Petrova's interiority section (lines 133-143), particularly the cat-death detail, is the collection's strongest rendering of expanded perception in a civilian context (voice-auditor, craft-auditor)
3. Diplomatic texture is precise and functional without being decorative -- badges, horseshoe seating, cream-colored agendas ground the fantastical in bureaucratic reality (craft-auditor)
4. Canonical consistency is tight across all factual claims -- casualty counts, document structure, character backgrounds, bandwidth system (consistency-auditor)
5. Harrington's two lines ("We stopped funding it. The researchers continued on their own for four months. Then we stopped them.") are among the best minor-character writing in the collection (voice-auditor)

**Key Issues:**
1. No character transformation -- the story is procedural, not dramatic (structure-auditor, craft-auditor, voice-auditor)
2. Movement IV deflates after the bar scene -- summary where scene is needed (craft-auditor, structure-auditor)
3. Napkin ending ("When, not if.") is a thriller convention in a literary-register story (craft-auditor, structure-auditor)
4. Over-explained diplomatic signals and Petrova's voice flattening during exposition (craft-auditor, voice-auditor)

**Finding Counts**: HIGH: 3 | MEDIUM: 5 | LOW: 5

## HIGH Issues

### H1: No character transformation (source: structure-auditor, craft-auditor, voice-auditor)
- **Location**: Entire story
- **Problem**: Neither Hayes nor Petrova changes during the story. Hayes arrives as a competent intelligence officer and leaves as one who has decided to act -- a pragmatic calculation, not a transformation. Petrova arrives performing normalcy, briefly drops the mask, and resumes. Compare to every other spinoff: Webb confronts the loss of everything he loves. Sophia confronts ambiguity she cannot resolve. Okafor assembles moral clarity and leaves. Even Mara, who understands nothing, is marked by the word "beautiful." The Vienna Accords is the only story in the collection where nobody's understanding of themselves shifts.
- **Suggestion**: The most promising vector is Hayes. The story should give her a micro-arc: **institutional confidence -> epistemic humility -> the decision to act despite incomprehension**. This requires three additions:
  1. An early beat (Movement I or II) where Hayes genuinely believes protocol can address this problem. Not naive -- measured professional confidence that international frameworks can contain the threat.
  2. A moment in the bar scene where Hayes does not just receive information from Petrova but *feels* the floor drop out -- the realization that the gap between what she understands and what Petrova perceives is not professional (a classification level) but ontological (a different kind of seeing). Hayes's intelligence training should detect this through Petrova's body language, the quality of her attention, the speed of her processing. Hayes should feel, for the first time, what it is to be outperceived.
  3. A departure beat (Movement IV) where Hayes carries something she did not carry before. The napkin, if rethought, could serve this: not a prediction ("When, not if") but an object representing the gap between what Hayes can know and what she has chosen to trust anyway.
- **Cross-verified**: Yes. All three non-consistency auditors identified this independently.

### H2: Movement IV does not function as a closing section (source: craft-auditor, structure-auditor)
- **Location**: Lines 253-291 (~800 words)
- **Quoted text**: "The accord was drafted in a sidebar room at the Vienna International Centre, between eleven p.m. and three in the morning, by eleven people who had stopped believing in the document's sufficiency around midnight..."
- **Problem**: Movement IV attempts to dramatize the drafting process and deliver the departure. At 800 words in summary mode, it achieves neither. No dialogue. No conflict. No moment where the accord might fail. The section reads as a report card on the negotiation rather than its culmination. After the sustained tension of the bar scene, the story needs either quick release (compress to denouement) or final escalation (dramatize a crisis within the drafting). The current section provides neither.
- **Suggestion**: Two options. **Option A (compress)**: Cut the drafting summary to 200 words. Accept the bar scene as the climax. Move directly to the departure, which becomes the emotional resolution via H1's arc completion. **Option B (dramatize)**: Expand one moment within the drafting into a real scene -- Zhang's technical annex, perhaps, rendered in dialogue, showing her building a sensor network out of diplomatic language while Hayes watches and recognizes the craft. This gives Movement IV its own dramatic interest and grounds the "skeleton" metaphor in a specific achievement.
- **Cross-verified**: Yes. Both the prior review (2026-03-19) and this review's craft and structure auditors identified this independently.

### H3: Napkin ending is generic (source: craft-auditor, structure-auditor)
- **Location**: Lines 283-289
- **Quoted text**: "a phone number with no country code, and two words: *When, not if.*"
- **Problem**: "When, not if" is a thriller convention. It makes a prediction. The novel's register is about uncertainty, about the gap between what can be known and what cannot, about holding questions rather than declaring answers. "When, not if" closes that gap. It does not fit the register of a story that has spent 5,000 words establishing the impossibility of knowing.
- **Suggestion**: Three options: (1) **Just the phone number, no words.** Petrova has already said everything in the bar. The absence of text IS the message. The phone number alone -- an unlabeled channel into the Order -- carries more weight than any words. (2) **A question instead of a statement.** Something that opens rather than closes, echoing the story's theme about the gap between perception and comprehension. (3) **A single word from the novel's vocabulary** that means nothing to Hayes now and everything later. The napkin should create mystery, not closure.
- **Cross-verified**: Yes. The prior review independently flagged this.

## MEDIUM Issues

### M1: Over-explained diplomatic signals (source: craft-auditor)
- **Location**: Lines 145-148
- **Quoted text**: "Two minutes late---not rudeness but calibration. Arriving on time would cede the positional advantage of having chosen the meeting. Arriving late asserted control of the schedule." and "the Scotch was a prop, a social object that said *I am here as a person, not only as an officer*. But not drinking it said *I am still working.*"
- **Problem**: Both passages decode diplomatic behavior for the reader. The story's register elsewhere trusts inference (Zhang's question works because the text does NOT explain "she asked this because she had seen it happen"). These passages violate that principle.
- **Suggestion**: Line 145: Cut the explanation after "calibration." Keep "Petrova noted the calculation and appreciated it." Line 147: Cut "Another signal:" through "*I am still working.*" The action speaks for itself.
- **Cross-verified**: Yes. Prior review flagged lines 147-148.

### M2: Petrova's voice flattens to briefing register during exposition (source: voice-auditor, craft-auditor)
- **Location**: Lines 207-221
- **Quoted text**: "Uncontrolled exposure. At scale. People interacting with systems whose outputs restructure cognitive processing, without knowing the outputs are doing it..."
- **Problem**: This is policy-brief language, not Petrova's established voice. Her canonical voice uses spatial metaphor and indirection ("the plumbing inside every wall"). When she shifts to institutional language, the voice distinction between Petrova and Hayes collapses. The exposition also runs long -- three consecutive Petrova speeches with Hayes reduced to short questions.
- **Suggestion**: Rewrite in Petrova's voice. Let her describe the commercial threat through what she perceives, not through policy abstraction. And break the exposition with Hayes's own expertise -- she is a DARPA officer, she knows about commercial AI scaling. Let her arrive at conclusions independently, with Petrova confirming or correcting. This transforms lecture into collaborative inference.
- **Cross-verified**: Yes. Both voice-auditor and craft-auditor identified this from different angles.

### M3: Caldwell's interior psychology in Hayes-limited POV (source: consistency-auditor)
- **Location**: Line 25
- **Quoted text**: "He believed in none of them. He believed in the process of saying them, which was not the same thing."
- **Problem**: This is a Hayes-limited-third section. Hayes can observe Caldwell's behavior but cannot access his beliefs. The phrasing presents his psychology as narratorial fact.
- **Suggestion**: Reframe as Hayes's inference: "He believed in none of them, Hayes judged." Or render through behavior: "The words passed through him and emerged unchanged."
- **Cross-verified**: No cross-verification needed. Clear POV discipline issue.

### M4: "Economy" repetition (source: consistency-auditor, craft-auditor)
- **Location**: Lines 51 and 125
- **Quoted text**: Line 51: "each step placed with the economy of someone who had calculated the path" / Line 125: "each step placed with the economy of a person who had already solved the path"
- **Problem**: Nearly identical phrasing 74 lines apart for the same character trait.
- **Suggestion**: Vary the second instance. The "same efficient movement" callback is already established; the observation should evolve. Perhaps the second time, Hayes notices purpose rather than efficiency, or habit rather than calculation.
- **Cross-verified**: Yes. Both consistency and craft auditors flagged this.

### M5: Petrova's age -- "mid-forties" vs. 47-48 (source: consistency-auditor)
- **Location**: Line 13
- **Quoted text**: "The woman was different. Mid-forties, dark hair cut short"
- **Problem**: Born 1971, in March 2019 Petrova is 47-48. "Mid-forties" implies 44-46.
- **Suggestion**: Change to "late forties" or remove the age descriptor entirely.
- **Cross-verified**: No. Factual arithmetic check; no second opinion needed.

## What Works (Do Not Break)

These elements are strong and should be preserved through revision:

1. **The opening line** ("The badge was the wrong shade of blue.") -- sets the register immediately.
2. **Movement II, the closed session** -- the escalation from Hayes's presentation through Zhang's question is excellent diplomatic fiction. Do not restructure.
3. **Zhang's question and Hayes's analysis** (lines 79-89) -- the story's best dramatic moment. The inference unfolds in real time.
4. **Petrova's interiority** (lines 133-143) -- the bar-reading, the BVT officer, the cat death. This is where the bandwidth gap becomes experiential rather than conceptual.
5. **Petrova's plumbing metaphor** (line 249) -- the perfect register for describing expanded perception to a normal-bandwidth listener.
6. **Harrington's two lines** (line 77) -- more institutional tragedy in two sentences than most fiction achieves in a chapter.
7. **The "could not unsuspect" construction** (line 101) -- precise and memorable.
8. **Hayes's operational discipline** -- her intelligence-officer register is consistent and convincing throughout. The revision should deepen it, not soften it. The transformation Hayes needs is not emotional vulnerability but epistemic humility: the realization that her competence has limits she cannot bridge.

## Revision Priorities

### Tier 1 (Structural -- do these first)
1. **Give Hayes an arc.** Add the three beats described in H1. This is the revision that would most dramatically improve the story.
2. **Rework Movement IV.** Either compress to pure denouement or dramatize one scene within the drafting (Option A or B from H2).
3. **Replace the napkin text.** Phone number alone, or a question, or a single resonant word. Not "When, not if."

### Tier 2 (Craft -- do these after structure is settled)
4. **Cut the diplomatic-signal explanations** (M1, lines 145-148).
5. **Rewrite Petrova's exposition in her established voice** (M2, lines 207-221).
6. **Fix the Caldwell POV break** (M3, line 25).
7. **Vary the "economy" repetition** (M4, lines 51/125).

### Tier 3 (Polish)
8. Fix Petrova's age (M5).
9. Address LOW-severity repetitions (L1-L5).
