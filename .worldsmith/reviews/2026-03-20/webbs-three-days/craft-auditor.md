# Craft Auditor Report: Webb's Three Days

**Date:** 2026-03-20
**Scope:** `spinoffs/webbs-three-days/chapters/day-one.tex`, `day-two.tex`, `day-three.tex` (~6,600 words)

---

## Overall Craft Assessment

The prose is consistently strong. This is among the best writing in the shared universe -- controlled, specific, and disciplined in what it withholds. The three-day structure provides natural escalation, and the prose itself tracks the escalation: Day 1 is measured and observational, Day 2 is warm and clinical through Rachel's lens, Day 3 compresses as Webb's cognition fails. The story earns its emotional payoff through accumulated physical detail rather than rhetorical declaration.

## MEDIUM Issues

### M1: Day 2 clinical catalogue is redundant (lines 149-151)

**Location:** day-two.tex:149-151
**Quoted text:** `"visual cortex recruitment, altered connectivity between ventral and dorsal streams, modified saccade programming, enhanced feature binding"`
**Problem:** This clinical catalogue repeats what Rachel has already demonstrated through direct observation. By this point in Day 2, the reader has watched Rachel notice saccade changes (line 29-33), motor optimization (lines 5-8), fixation patterns (line 43-47), pupil dilation (line 51), and sleep architecture changes (lines 137-143). The list adds nothing the prose hasn't already shown. It converts demonstrated expertise into stated expertise.
**Suggestion:** Cut lines 149-151 ("Her career was built on understanding how training reshapes vision. She had documented, in paper after paper, the specific cortical changes..." through "enhanced feature binding"). The preceding sentence ("She was a perceptual scientist") is sufficient. The paragraph that follows ("What she was observing in James was all of this. Every signature.") can link directly to "Which meant one of two things."
**Confidence:** HIGH.

### M2: Web-search sequence shifts from experiential to expository (Day 3, lines 119-131)

**Location:** day-three.tex:119-131
**Quoted text:** Starting from `"He searched."` through the Foundation for Perceptual Sciences website.
**Problem:** The story maintains an experiential, inside-the-perception register for 5,500 words. The web-search sequence breaks this register with a plot-mechanics passage: search terms in italics, research results summarized, a convenient paper by "A. Chen" with a phone number. The tonal shift is noticeable. The Willoughby Britton reference (line 123) works because it describes the phenomenological parallel Webb feels. The "A. Chen" paper and Foundation website (lines 127-131) feel like thriller plotting -- too convenient, too tidy.
**Suggestion:** The prior review flagged this. The current version is better than a straightforward Google-solves-everything sequence, but the Foundation website with "almost no content" and a phone number still reads as plot machinery. Consider: Webb finds the paper, recognizes his experience in the vocabulary, and the paper's bibliography leads to an email address he writes to at 2 AM. The call comes FROM the Order the next morning. This preserves the Order's institutional intelligence (they monitor for people who find the breadcrumbs) rather than making Webb the active finder.
**Confidence:** MEDIUM -- the current version works well enough and is consistent with the lore's description of the Order seeding academic literature.

### M3: "Beautiful terrible" adjective stacking (Day 3, line 23)

**Location:** day-three.tex:23
**Quoted text:** `"the same beautiful terrible clockwork of human habit"`
**Problem:** "Beautiful terrible" without a comma reads as a verbal tic the parent novel avoids. The adjective-adjective construction without punctuation is a specific craft pattern the style guide warns against.
**Suggestion:** Either "the same beautiful, terrible clockwork" (comma) or rephrase: "the same merciless clockwork of human habit."
**Confidence:** HIGH.

### M4: Repetition of "the way" comparative construction

**Location:** Multiple instances across all three days
**Observation:** The phrase "the way you [verb]" appears approximately 8 times across 6,600 words:
- day-one.tex:55 "the way you notice your own breathing"
- day-one.tex:59 "the way you perceive the approximate number"
- day-one.tex:71 "the way you perceive the skeleton of a building"
- day-one.tex:77 "the way the bolt count had arrived on BART"
- day-two.tex:63 "the way she'd signal her lab students"
- day-two.tex:81 "the way Rachel parsed"
- day-three.tex:13 "the way you feel sunlight on your skin"
- day-three.tex:111 "the way you feel a burn"
**Problem:** Eight instances in 6,600 words (one per ~825 words) is frequent enough to become a visible pattern. The construction works well for Webb's dual-perception motif (comparing the unfamiliar to the familiar), but the repetition risks becoming a stylistic tic rather than a deliberate device.
**Suggestion:** Reduce to 5-6 instances. The Day 1 instances are densest (4 in ~1,700 words) and could lose one or two. Consider varying the comparative construction: "like perceiving..." or "as though..." for some instances.
**Confidence:** MEDIUM.

## LOW Issues

### L1: "Three per panel" is redundant (Day 1, line 59)

**Location:** day-one.tex:59
**Quoted text:** `"Twenty-seven. Eighty-one bolts. Three per panel."`
**Problem:** The reader can compute 81/27 = 3. The explicit statement "Three per panel" undercuts the involuntary nature of Webb's counting by making it explanatory rather than perceptual. The horror is that the number arrives pre-formed; explaining the ratio domesticates it.
**Suggestion:** Cut "Three per panel." End on "Eighty-one bolts." The unasked-for precision is more unsettling without the explanation.
**Confidence:** HIGH.

### L2: Minor over-specificity in cat purring (Day 3, line 75)

**Location:** day-three.tex:75
**Quoted text:** `"25 hertz, the frequency that promotes bone density, evolved as a self-healing mechanism in small felids"`
**Problem:** This reads as a parenthetical trivia note rather than perceptual experience. Webb is perceiving everything as data, which is correct for his Day 3 state, but the "promotes bone density, evolved as a self-healing mechanism" clause sounds like a Wikipedia entry rather than involuntary pattern recognition. The 25 hertz number is fine; the evolutionary explanation is too discursive for Webb's fragmenting state.
**Suggestion:** Trim to: `"The cat's purring was a vibration---25 hertz---and beneath it the structure of fur, each hair a keratin filament with specific geometric properties---"` The evolutionary detail can be cut without loss.
**Confidence:** MEDIUM.

### L3: Phone-checking man echoes Day 1 and Day 3

**Location:** day-one.tex:61 (first appearance), day-three.tex:23 (callback)
**Observation:** The man checking his phone every forty seconds appears in both Day 1 (BART) and Day 3 (BART again). This is a strong structural callback -- same man, same compulsion, different Webb. The callback works. No fix needed. Noting as a craft strength.

### L4: Pattern count check

**Observation:**
- "something": 19 instances (~2.9 per 1k words). Acceptable; below the novel's reduced ceiling.
- "just": 7 instances. All thematic (normalcy contrast or emotional directness). None are filter words.
- "could see/feel/hear": ~12 instances. Most are deliberate dual-perception motif. Acceptable density for this story's particular thematic concerns.
- Dialogue tags: 1 "murmured" (Rachel stirring, appropriate). No "said quietly," "whispered," or "muttered." Clean.
- "felt a [emotion noun]": Zero instances. Clean.

1. **day-two.tex:127-129** -- The dual-perception thesis sentence. Single-sentence tour de force that earns its length through accumulating clauses. Each phrase adds a new layer without losing the syntactic thread.

2. **day-one.tex:69-77** -- The breathing observation scene. Physical detail grounds abstract perception. The architecture metaphor ("perceive the skeleton of a building through its facade") is precise and specific to Webb's character.

3. **day-three.tex:105-109** -- Webb's "I wish they would" speech. The emotional logic is devastating: wishing for the loss of feeling because feeling is what makes the loss unbearable. This is the story's thematic center and it earns its position through 5,000 words of preparation.

4. **day-three.tex:159** -- The final sentence. Earns its weight. "Between these two points" structurally mirrors Webb's dual perception throughout.
