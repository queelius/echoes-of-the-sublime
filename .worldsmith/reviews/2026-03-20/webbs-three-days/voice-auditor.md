## MEDIUM Issues

### M1: Day 1 BART observation briefly reads as Lena-register (line 61)

**Location:** day-one.tex:61
**Quoted text:** `"A man in a suit checking his phone with the mechanical regularity of someone who checks his phone every forty seconds. (Forty seconds. Webb counted three intervals. Forty, forty-one, forty. He hadn't tried to count.)"`
**Problem:** The parenthetical observation -- counting intervals, noting the precision, the involuntary quality of the counting -- is exactly how Lena would narrate in the novel. Both characters are cognitive scientists; both notice patterns. But Webb's register should be warmer, more embedded in the human scene. Lena would note the "mechanical regularity" as data. Webb should notice the man's compulsion as something he recognizes -- a human behavior that is now visible as pattern. The difference is empathy: Webb sees the humanity inside the pattern; Lena sees the pattern and files the humanity.
**Suggestion:** Add a micro-beat of recognition: the man isn't just a data point but someone caught in his own loop. E.g., `"A man in a suit checking his phone with the regularity of someone who doesn't know he checks his phone every forty seconds. (Forty, forty-one, forty. Webb hadn't tried to count. The man hadn't tried to check. Both of them automated.)"` This preserves the involuntary counting while adding the empathic layer that distinguishes Webb from Lena.
**Confidence:** MEDIUM -- the current version works and is internally consistent; the improvement would deepen Webb's specific character voice.

### M2: Rachel's voice is strong but could differentiate further from Lena

**Location:** day-two.tex throughout
**Observation:** Rachel's voice is well-constructed: clinical when analyzing, warm when feeling, with clear transitions between modes. The perceptual-science vocabulary (saccades, fixation patterns, perceptual learning, cortical plasticity) is appropriate and distinct from Lena's neuroscience vocabulary. However, Rachel and Lena share a structural similarity: both are female cognitive scientists observing something beyond their framework. The differentiation comes primarily through vocabulary and emotional engagement (Rachel feels; Lena doesn't). This works, but Rachel's voice could benefit from one more distinguishing trait.
**Suggestion:** Rachel's voice could carry a specific intellectual habit that Lena does not share. The lore-notes specify that Rachel studied perceptual learning in radiologists and chess players. This expertise could manifest as a verbal tic: Rachel reaching for clinical analogies ("the way a radiologist develops..." "like chess expertise..." "in my lab, we'd call this...") that she catches herself doing. This would give her voice a specific texture distinct from Lena's more generalized clinical register. The story already has one instance of this (day-two.tex:47: "She heard herself shifting into clinical register and stopped") -- this self-awareness is Rachel's distinguishing trait and could appear once or twice more.
**Confidence:** LOW-MEDIUM -- the current voice works well; this is an enrichment suggestion.

### M3: Day 3 fragmentation could escalate more gradually

**Location:** day-three.tex:1-20 vs. day-three.tex:83-100
**Observation:** The Day 3 fragmentation specified in the story-notes should escalate gradually: early Day 3 shows compression (subjects dropping, parenthetical cascades); late Day 3 shows fragmentation (sentences restarting, threads lost, emotional statements remaining whole). The manuscript mostly achieves this, but the early Day 3 prose (lines 1-20) is already quite compressed ("BART at seven. Shouldn't go in. Knew this. Went.") -- this is perhaps a beat too fragmented for the start of Day 3. The fragmentary style peaks appropriately in the conversation with Rachel (lines 83-100), but the contrast between early and late Day 3 could be sharper.
**Suggestion:** Give lines 1-20 slightly more syntactic completeness. E.g., "He took BART at seven. He shouldn't have gone in. He knew this. He went anyway." This preserves compression while keeping subjects and connective tissue intact. The full fragmentation (dropped subjects, collapsed clauses) can arrive after the second session.
**Confidence:** MEDIUM.
