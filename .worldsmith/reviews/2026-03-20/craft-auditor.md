# Craft Audit: Sophia's Butterflies

**Date**: 2026-03-20
**Scope**: Full manuscript (`spinoffs/sophias-butterflies/chapters/sophia.tex`, ~3,800 words)
**Style guide checked**: lore/style-guide.md, spinoff lore/characters.md (voice calibration)

---

## Strengths

1. **The hand-movement ambiguity** (lines 143-167): Masterfully constructed. Both readings (real response vs. motor noise) are given full prose weight. The paragraph break at line 153 ("Except the hand.") is devastating in its brevity. The clinical rebuttal (line 147) is as convincing as the emotional reading (lines 145-146). Neither wins.

2. **The fourth-quadrant solution** (lines 209-217): The recursive butterfly -- the simple crayon drawing inside the mathematical rendering -- works simultaneously as math (self-reference in the Mandelbrot set), as emotion (innocence persisting inside sophistication), and as structural closure (the story's recursion theme made visual). This is the story's strongest craft move.

3. **The opening image system**: Ravens, data, the gap between Sophia's counting and Maya's pattern-finding (lines 3-7). Sets up the entire story's tension between observation and meaning in seven lines.

4. **Register control**: The code-switching between Sophia's narration and her speech is precise. "Fine" means "stop asking" (line 15). "Same" means "same" (line 197). The narration runs long, analytical, mathematical. The dialogue runs short, declarative, emotionally compressed.

5. **The stack of butterflies** (line 175): "crayon addition, marker fractions, colored-pencil geometry, ink-and-ruler trigonometry, the careful graphite of set theory" -- a girl's cognitive development compressed into art media. Economical and moving.

6. **"The truth of the statement does not depend on its reception"** (line 185): The story's thesis, delivered as mathematical logic, felt as love. The dual register is perfectly calibrated.

## Findings

### CR-M1: "I think I got that from you" -- Register Break (MEDIUM)

**Location**: Line 93
**Quoted text**: "I think I got that from you."
**Problem**: This is too soft for Sophia's established voice. Throughout the story, Sophia is sharp, precise, unsoftened: "I know you're not dead. I know the difference" (line 97). "But you chose this" (line 101). "I think I got that from you" uses hedging ("I think") and passive construction ("got that from you") that undercut her sharpness. It reads as a 30-year-old reflecting gently, not a 16-year-old with cold anger.
**Suggestion**: Replace with something harder and more mathematical: "That's yours. The structural approach. The instinct to start from the whole and work inward. I inherited it the way I inherited your hair." Or shorter: "That's yours. I know it's yours." The hardness should be present even in moments of connection.
**Confidence**: High. The user flagged this independently, and the voice calibration notes support it.

### CR-M2: "Looking back" Over-Explanation (MEDIUM)

**Location**: Line 187
**Quoted text**: "She walks out of the ward without looking back. This is new---she used to look back, used to stand in the doorway trying to catch her mother's eyes shifting toward her, trying to extract one more second of proximity. She stopped last year. Looking back was just another way of searching for signal in noise, and she has decided that the looking was damaging her in ways she couldn't afford."
**Problem**: The first two sentences are strong -- the behavioral change ("this is new") and the old habit ("used to stand in the doorway"). But the explanation ("Looking back was just another way of searching for signal in noise") over-explains what the reader has already understood from the hand-movement scene. Sophia herself has just spent 20 lines analyzing her own perception of signal vs. noise. The reader doesn't need the explicit connection.
**Suggestion**: Cut from "Looking back was just another way..." to the end. End the paragraph on "She stopped last year." The reader will connect it. If a beat is needed, a physical action: "She stopped last year. Her hands find the bag strap and grip."
**Confidence**: High. The user flagged this independently. The style guide says "trust the reader to infer connections."

### CR-M3: Final Run-On Sentence (MEDIUM)

**Location**: Line 221
**Quoted text**: "...which is a physical process she can describe, which is math, which is beautiful, which is a thing her mother taught her, which is a thing she will not let go of, which is a thing she will bring back next month, on wings."
**Problem**: Six "which is" clauses in a single sentence. The cascading structure is intended as a breathless emotional surge, and the first four work well (physical process -> math -> beautiful -> mother taught her). But clauses 5 and 6 lose momentum. "A thing she will not let go of" is abstract after the concrete "a thing her mother taught her." And "a thing she will bring back next month, on wings" restates what the reader already knows.
**Suggestion**: Cut to 4 clauses. End on "which is a thing her mother taught her" and let the image land. Or keep 5 and cut the weakest: "which is a physical process she can describe, which is math, which is beautiful, which is a thing her mother taught her, which is a thing she will bring back next month, on wings." The "let go" clause is the weakest because it tells rather than shows.
**Confidence**: High. The user flagged this independently (said 7, actual count is 6). Four or five would land harder.

### CR-M4: Monologue Length (MEDIUM)

**Location**: Lines 97-105
**Quoted text**: The anger monologue beginning "I also think that's what killed you..." through "And the worst part is that I understand why..."
**Problem**: This is approximately 350 words of unbroken speech. Sophia is speaking to a catatonic patient in a quiet ward. The content is strong -- the anger, the accusation, the admission of understanding -- but the sustained length strains credibility for spoken-aloud dialogue, even a monologue. A 16-year-old delivering this without pause, stumble, or physical break is performing a speech, not talking to her mother. The voice calibration notes identify this concern: "currently serves as the story's emotional core but runs ~400 words of unbroken speech."
**Suggestion**: Insert 2-3 physical breaks. After "But you chose this" (line 101), a breath, a glance at Maya's face, a physical reaction (adjusting the chair, touching the drawing). After "which is saying something, because this building is terrifying" (line 105), a pause where she runs out of words and has to gather herself. These breaks let the monologue breathe and make Sophia's control feel more earned.
**Confidence**: Medium. The monologue is effective on the page, but its delivery in-world stretches plausibility.

### CR-L1: "Something" Count (LOW)

**Location**: Throughout (14 instances)
**Quoted text**: Various -- "something between the two" (line 15), "One types something" (line 37), "tracking something invisible" (line 53), "I brought you something" (line 71), etc.
**Problem**: 14 instances in ~3,800 words is a rate of approximately 1 per 271 words. The parent novel has been reduced to ~80 instances across ~73,000 words (~1 per 912 words). The spinoff's rate is 3.4x higher. Several are deliberate and effective (line 15's "something between the two" names the unnameable grief; line 145's "Something else" marks the hand movement). Others are filler: "One types something" (line 37), "Something crosses the woman's face" (line 189).
**Suggestion**: Cut 3-4 of the filler instances. Line 37: "One types." Line 189: "A look crosses the woman's face." Keep all thematically loaded uses.
**Confidence**: Medium. Some are clearly deliberate; the filler instances could be trimmed.

### CR-L2: "Just" Count (LOW)

**Location**: Lines 41, 91, 187
**Quoted text**: "It just burns" (41), "She just knows" (91), "just another way" (187)
**Problem**: Three instances in 3,800 words. Rate is consistent with the parent novel's post-reduction level (~94 in 73k words). Line 91 ("She just knows") is the weakest -- "She knows" is stronger without the qualifier.
**Suggestion**: Cut line 91's "just." Keep the other two (line 41 works as emphasis; line 187 works if the over-explanation is trimmed per CR-M2).
**Confidence**: Low. Minor.

### CR-L3: Math Exposition Density (LOW)

**Location**: Lines 77, 133-137
**Quoted text**: The Mandelbrot/Julia set descriptions, including specific parameter ($c = -0.7 + 0.27i$) and the iterated function notation.
**Problem**: The mathematical detail is accurate and serves characterization (Sophia thinks in math). But the LaTeX-rendered formula ($f(z) = z^2 + c$) and the specific complex parameter may create a speed bump for non-mathematical readers. The progress notes flag this: "The Mandelbrot/Julia set math is accurate but may need trimming if it slows the emotional momentum."
**Suggestion**: Keep the Mandelbrot/Julia set framing but consider cutting the specific parameter value. "The corresponding Julia set at a parameter she chose because the resulting shape looks like neural branching" conveys the same meaning without the $c = -0.7 + 0.27i$. The iterated function is fine -- it's brief.
**Confidence**: Low. This is a stylistic choice. The math is part of Sophia's voice. For literary fiction readers, it may slow; for the shared-universe audience, it enriches.
