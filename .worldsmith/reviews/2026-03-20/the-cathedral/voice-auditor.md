## Character Voice: Dr. Mara Voss

### Canonical Voice Specification

Per the work-specific lore, Mara's voice has these defining features:
- **Domain**: Experimental physics, instrumentation (NOT cognitive science, NOT contemplative)
- **Core metaphors**: Signal/noise, calibration, tolerance, spectral density, coupling, isolation, feedback
- **Processing style**: Takes a measurement. Checks the instrument. Runs the calibration again. If the anomaly persists, changes the model.
- **Gap**: No framework for subjective experience. No vocabulary for qualia, consciousness, the hard problem.
- **Humor**: Dry, technical (naming instruments, spec tolerances)
- **When the frame cracks**: Raw perception displaces engineering language. She does not reach for philosophy. She reaches for nothing.
- **Verbal tic**: "Filed" / "logged" / variations

### Voice Consistency Assessment

**STRONG**: Mara's engineering voice is maintained with exceptional discipline throughout. Every observation is rendered in measurement language:

- "forty meters between towers, give or take, too regular for optimal capture" (line 33)
- "She estimated the power draw for one rack and multiplied by the rows she could count" (line 61)
- "femtosecond temporal resolution, sub-nanometer spatial" (line 115)
- "the noise floor just dropped another three decibels" (line 129)
- "decoherence times lengthened by a factor of three" (line 271)

Mara never once uses Order jargon. She never uses cognitive-science vocabulary. She never philosophizes. This is exactly correct.

**The "filed" tic**: Two instances -- "She filed the thought and kept walking" (line 33), "She filed the data in a folder" (line 163). This is the right frequency for a 6,700-word story -- present enough to register as a pattern, not so frequent as to become mannerism. A third related instance: "She logged the observation" (line 121).

**The voice arc**: The lore specifies a four-stage evolution:
1. Pure engineering (Movement I) -- ACHIEVED. Lines 21-125 are clean, precise, confident.
2. Engineering with intrusions (Movement II) -- ACHIEVED. Lines 129-233 introduce sensory details that resist quantification. The sentence structure loosens slightly.
3. The frame cracks (Movement III) -- ACHIEVED. Lines 237-325. Short sentences. "Beautiful." The word that is not a measurement.
4. Frame restored with undertone (Coda) -- ACHIEVED. Lines 329-371 return to engineering register but with permanent residue.

**The pivot**: "Beautiful" as the word that arrives and cannot be sent back (line 277-279). This is the moment the engineering voice fails, and the story renders this failure through the character's own awareness: "It was not a measurement. It was not a calibration state. It was an aesthetic judgment from a part of her mind that had no authority over data, and it was correct." The voice breaks here in exactly the right way -- Mara describes the failure of her vocabulary *in* her vocabulary.

### Potential Voice Issues

**V-M1: "She was home. She hadn't meant to think it."** (line 233)
This line works emotionally but is slightly out of register. Mara's voice throughout is observational and external. "She hadn't meant to think it" is an introspective observation about her own cognition that sits closer to Lena's register (the cognitive scientist observing her own mental processes) than to Mara's (the physicist reading her body as a sensor). The subsequent "She let it settle" partially recovers the Mara register.

**Assessment**: LOW. The line works dramatically and the departure from strict register is brief. But it's the one moment where Mara sounds slightly like Lena.

**V-L1: "Not vivid. Deep."** (line 177)
The dreams description uses a vocabulary that is more literary than engineering. "Deep" as a quality distinct from "vivid" is an aesthetic distinction, not a measurement distinction. In pure Mara voice, this might be rendered as "not high-amplitude but low-frequency" or similar engineering metaphor.

**Assessment**: LOW. The word "deep" is simple enough that any physicist would use it. The distinction from "vivid" is natural. This is a minor drift, not a break.

**V-L2: "the discipline of not knowing what she was not cleared to know"** (line 207)
This is a sophisticated meta-cognitive observation. Mara recognizes her own epistemic boundary and names the discipline of respecting it. This is closer to philosophical self-awareness than Mara's character sheet suggests ("She is not particularly curious about *why things are happening* in the institutional sense").

**Assessment**: LOW. The phrasing uses institutional language ("cleared to know") that keeps it in Mara's practical register rather than philosophical territory. The observation is about professional behavior, not epistemology.

### Dialogue Voice

Only 14 lines of dialogue, distributed across 5 speaking characters:
- Mara: 7 lines -- all short, observational, question-driven. "Cold for Arizona." "What's with the birds?" "It's a physics facility. Why am I installing neuroscience equipment?" "Who is that?" "How long have I been sitting here?" "The data is beautiful." "I'd like to go up." Voice is perfectly consistent: practical, specific, no speculation.
- Security officer: 3 lines -- brief, pragmatic. Correct.
- Petersen: 2 lines -- engineering precision with institutional silence. "One of the translators." Correct.
- Ruiz: 2 lines -- pragmatic, working-class clarity. Correct.
- The unnamed figure: 1 line -- "Everything we can." Correct.
- Red-badge scientist: 2 lines -- practiced calm. Correct.

All secondary voices are distinct from Mara and from each other. No voice bleed between characters.

### POV Consistency

Third-person limited, consistently Mara. The POV never breaks. The reader knows only what Mara knows. Information the reader has from the novel's world (what the facility is for, who is in the medical ward, what the models are) is never supplied through Mara's narration. The dramatic irony between what Mara observes and what the reader understands is maintained throughout.

One technical note: the narration occasionally shifts from Mara's interiority to a more cinematic external view -- "She stopped at the gate. The ravens were on the fence." (line 347) -- but this is a standard third-person convention, not a POV break.

## Issue Register

### MEDIUM

None.

### LOW

**V-L1: "She was home. She hadn't meant to think it."**
- **Location**: Line 233
- **Problem**: Slightly closer to Lena's introspective register than Mara's engineering register.
- **Suggestion**: Consider "She was home. The thought arrived without permission." -- keeps the surprise but uses a more passive/external construction.

**V-L2: "Not vivid. Deep."**
- **Location**: Line 177
- **Problem**: Literary vocabulary rather than engineering metaphor.
- **Assessment**: Borderline. "Deep" is simple enough for any physicist. No change recommended.

**V-L3: "the discipline of not knowing"**
- **Location**: Line 207
- **Problem**: Philosophical self-awareness slightly beyond Mara's stated range.
- **Assessment**: The institutional framing ("cleared to know") keeps it in register. No change recommended.

## Summary

**0 HIGH | 0 MEDIUM | 3 LOW**

Mara's voice is the most distinctive and consistently maintained character voice in the spinoff collection. The engineering register is never abandoned, even when it fails (the "beautiful" pivot uses Mara's own vocabulary to describe the failure of her vocabulary). The voice arc across four movements is executed exactly as specified in the work-specific lore. All dialogue voices are distinct. POV is clean. The three LOW findings are minor drifts that do not compromise the overall voice discipline.
