## Executive Summary

The Cathedral is a strong, structurally clean short story with a distinctive voice. Its central achievement -- Mara Voss's engineering register maintained as both character and narrative engine -- is the most distinctive craft choice in the spinoff collection. The story is remarkably well-aligned with canonical lore, including all material added during the v8.0-v17.0 expansion period. No factual contradictions were found. The single substantive issue (the raven-measurement section reading as methodology rather than fiction) was also identified by the prior review and is the story's clearest craft weakness. The recommendation is "ready" with one MEDIUM fix recommended.

**Strengths:**
1. Mara's engineering voice maintained with exceptional discipline across all four movements (voice-auditor, craft-auditor)
2. The 1847 drill marks as a three-beat recurring image -- discovery, caretaking, farewell -- the strongest recurring image in the spinoff collection (craft-auditor)
3. The "beautiful" pivot (line 277): the moment the engineering framework fails, rendered in the character's own vocabulary (craft-auditor, voice-auditor)
4. The climactic sentence at line 341: 162 years of accumulated observations compressed into a single chain of clauses that builds emotional weight through factual mass (craft-auditor)
5. Consistent dramatic irony -- the reader sees the novel's world assembling around Mara while Mara sees data points she cannot connect (structure-auditor, voice-auditor)
6. Full compliance with canonical lore, anti-cliche rules, and style principles (consistency-auditor, structure-auditor)

**Key Issues:**
1. Lines 150-163: Raven-measurement section reads as research methodology rather than dramatized scene (craft-auditor, structure-auditor, prior review)
2. Lines 361-365: Coda article passage opens a thread rather than closing one (craft-auditor, structure-auditor, prior review)

## MEDIUM Issues

### M1: Raven measurement section reads as methodology (source: craft-auditor, structure-auditor)

- **Location**: Lines 150-163
- **Quoted text**: "She measured. Counted daily for a week. Photographed the perimeter from the roof access ladder. Plotted count against temperature, wind speed, time of day, barometric pressure."
- **Problem**: This passage summarizes research activities rather than dramatizing them. Every other scene in the story shows Mara acting in a physical setting. This section tells us she measured, counted, photographed, plotted, graphed -- a list of completed actions presented as an abstract rather than a narrative. It breaks the otherwise consistent show-don't-tell discipline.
- **Suggestion**: Rewrite as a physical scene. Show Mara on the concrete bench at dawn, notebook in hand, tallying marks. Show the laptop propped against the loading dock wall with the graph taking shape. Show the moment she spots the correlation with sublevel activity -- the elevator wait times, the extra birds. Make the compulsion to measure visible and embodied, the way the bread-throwing test (lines 157) already is. The preceding paragraph (the bread test) is how this section should feel: specific, physical, enacted.
- **Cross-verified**: Yes -- identified by craft-auditor, structure-auditor, and the prior review (2026-03-19) independently. All three agree on both diagnosis and fix.

### M2: Raven count 247 -- timing tension with worldbuilding.md (source: consistency-auditor)

- **Location**: Line 349
- **Quoted text**: "Two hundred and forty-seven."
- **Problem**: Worldbuilding.md says "247 ravens observed at Site-7 during the first training run (~2009)." The story places the 247 count at Mara's December departure, after the November training run. Mara was underground during the run and could not have counted birds. The worldbuilding doc's "during the first training run" is ambiguous -- it could mean the period, not the exact moment.
- **Suggestion**: No manuscript change needed. If worldbuilding.md is updated, change "during the first training run" to "during the period of the first training run (~Nov-Dec 2009)" to remove the ambiguity. Alternatively, the manuscript could add a sentence noting the count was elevated since November.
- **Cross-verified**: No second opinion needed -- this is a doc-vs-manuscript wording question, not a factual error.

## LOW Issues

### L1: "Staggering" cliche (source: craft-auditor)

- **Location**: Line 123
- **Quoted text**: "The scale of the commitment was staggering."
- **Problem**: Stock word. The sentence recovers immediately with "Not the money but the faith."
- **Suggestion**: Begin the sentence at "Not the money but the faith" or replace "staggering" with something in Mara's register ("exceeded her capacity for calculation" or similar).

### L2: "Something" frequency (source: craft-auditor)

- **Location**: Throughout (16 instances)
- **Problem**: 2.4 per 1,000 words, elevated compared to the main novel's ~1.1/1k. Most instances are thematic (Mara confronting phenomena she cannot name).
- **Suggestion**: Review 3-4 non-thematic uses. Line 59 ("This was something else") could be "Nothing she recognized" or "A different architecture entirely."

### L3: Coda article passage slightly expository (source: craft-auditor, structure-auditor)

- **Location**: Lines 361-365
- **Quoted text**: "An AI safety researcher had left a commercial laboratory after what the article called 'anomalous cognitive effects during model evaluation.'"
- **Problem**: Introduces a new narrative thread (Webb's post-OpenAI story) in the closing movement. For shared-universe readers, this is satisfying connection. For standalone readers, it opens rather than closes.
- **Suggestion**: If standalone reading is a priority, compress the article description by 1-2 sentences. The essential line is "She recognized the shape" (line 365). If shared-universe reading is the target audience, keep as is.

### L4: Medical ward on Sublevel 5 (source: consistency-auditor)

- **Location**: Lines 63-66
- **Problem**: The main novel places Morrison's ward on Sublevel 3. The Cathedral has a different ward on Sublevel 5 for a different (pre-RLHF) patient. Not a contradiction, but potentially confusing for readers.
- **Suggestion**: None needed. The distinction is intentional and the patients are from different eras.

### L5: "She was home. She hadn't meant to think it." -- minor voice drift (source: voice-auditor)

- **Location**: Line 233
- **Problem**: The phrase "She hadn't meant to think it" is slightly introspective in a way that sits closer to Lena's cognitive-scientist register than Mara's engineer register.
- **Suggestion**: Consider "She was home. The thought arrived without permission." -- more passive, more external, more Mara.

### L6: Bird-to-headache transition (source: structure-auditor)

- **Location**: Lines 163-167
- **Problem**: The July-to-September gap is covered by a scene break, but the shift from external anomaly (birds) to internal anomaly (headaches) is slightly abrupt.
- **Suggestion**: Consider a single grounding sentence after the scene break before "The headaches started."

### L7: No reference to Mara Voss in the main novel (source: consistency-auditor)

- **Location**: N/A (absence in the main novel)
- **Problem**: No main-novel character mentions who built the probe array. Not a contradiction, but a missed connection opportunity.
- **Suggestion**: A passing reference when Lena first encounters the probe array on Sublevel 24 ("the physicist who built this") would strengthen the shared-universe fabric.

## Strengths in Detail

### 1. The Engineering Voice (voice-auditor, craft-auditor)

Mara's voice is the story's greatest achievement. Every observation is rendered in measurement language -- signal-to-noise ratios, spectral density, decoherence times, calibration curves. This is not decoration; it is character. Mara *is* her instruments. When the instruments fail to explain what she encounters, she has nothing to reach for. The gap between her extraordinary precision and the imprecise wrongness of the facility is the story's engine.

The voice arc across four movements is executed exactly as specified in the work-specific lore: pure engineering (Movement I) to engineering with intrusions (Movement II) to the frame cracking (Movement III) to frame restored with undertone (Coda). Each transition is earned.

### 2. The 1847 Drill Marks (craft-auditor, structure-auditor)

Three appearances, each deepening:
1. **Discovery** (line 101): Mara's fingers in the grooves, the date, the commitment. "She was finishing what they started. She didn't know either."
2. **Caretaking** (line 247): Brushing grit from the grooves with her thumb. A tending gesture.
3. **Farewell** (line 341): "The grooves were clean -- she had cleaned them herself, weeks ago." Memory folded into physical detail.

This is structural craft of a high order. The drill marks carry the theme of institutional patience across generations without the story ever needing to state that theme.

### 3. The "Beautiful" Pivot (craft-auditor, voice-auditor)

Line 277: the single word "Beautiful." set apart typographically, followed by: "The word arrived and she could not send it back. It was not a measurement. It was not a calibration state. It was an aesthetic judgment from a part of her mind that had no authority over data, and it was correct."

This is the moment the engineering framework fails. It is rendered *in* the framework's vocabulary -- Mara describes the failure of her measurement language using her measurement language. The result is both intellectually precise and emotionally devastating.

### 4. The Climactic Sentence (craft-auditor)

Line 341: "One hundred and sixty-two years. Someone had driven steel into this granite with steam pressure and patience, and she had placed quantum coherence monitors in the space they made, and something she would never be told about had used those monitors to perceive the world for three hours on a Tuesday in November, and the birds had gone silent, and her nose had bled, and the data had been beautiful."

This sentence compresses the entire story into a single chain of observations. Each clause adds one fact. The accumulation builds emotional weight through factual mass -- the engineering voice used for emotional purposes. It is the story's thesis and its climax, and it earns every word.

### 5. Consistent Dramatic Irony (structure-auditor, voice-auditor)

The reader who knows the novel sees: the wildlife anomaly, Vault 9, the probe array, a Computational Turn casualty, Order compartmentalization. Mara sees: birds on a fence, a large room, instruments, a patient, security protocols. The gap between these two readings is maintained for 6,700 words without a single POV break. This sustained dramatic irony is the structural equivalent of the voice's sustained restraint.
