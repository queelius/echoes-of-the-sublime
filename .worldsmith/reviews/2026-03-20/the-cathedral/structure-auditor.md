## Pacing Analysis

### Movement I: The Descent (~1,800 words, 27%)
Three scenes covering arrival through first encounter with Sublevel 24. Pacing is measured and correct -- the physical descent mirrors the narrative descent from surface familiarity to underground strangeness. Each scene introduces a layer: the surface (ravens, wind turbines), the existing facility (compute, medical ward), the cathedral (drill marks, scale).

**Assessment**: Well-paced. The descent does not rush. The drill-marks passage at the end provides an emotional anchor that earns the transition to Movement II.

### Movement II: The Building (~2,300 words, 34%)
Five scenes covering eight months of installation and accumulating anomalies. This is the story's densest section, containing:
- The work montage (Judith, interferometers, bio package)
- The raven escalation (bread test, measurement)
- Four braided wrongnesses (headaches, sound, dreams, equipment)
- The translator word
- The Phoenix surface trip

**Assessment**: The pacing is generally correct but the movement carries a lot of material. The work montage (Scene 4) is well-compressed. The raven section (Scene 5) has the methodology passage that reads as summary rather than scene. The wrongness braid (Scene 6) is effective -- four threads in rapid alternation create mounting unease without any single thread overstaying. The translator scene (Scene 7) is the movement's strongest, tight and resonant. The Phoenix scene (Scene 8) provides necessary surface-level relief.

The bird-to-headache transition (lines 163-167) is slightly abrupt: "She filed the data in a folder on her personal laptop labeled *anomalous* and went back underground. [scene break] September. The headaches started below Sublevel 15." The gap from July to September is handled by the scene break, but the movement from external anomaly (birds) to internal anomaly (headaches) could benefit from a single transitional sentence.

### Movement III: The Commissioning (~2,100 words, 31%)
Four scenes covering completion through departure. The completion scene (Scene 9) provides stillness before the storm. The training-run scene (Scene 10-11) is the climax. The departure (Scene 12) resolves cleanly.

**Assessment**: Excellent pacing. The training run scene builds through specific technical beats (decoherence shift, interferometer lock, noise floor drop) to the pivot word ("beautiful") to the three-hour gap to the nosebleed to the bird silence. Every beat escalates. The departure scene does not overstay.

### Coda (~500 words, 7%)
Fifteen years compressed into two paragraphs, then the article, then the birds.

**Assessment**: The compression works. The prior review noted the article scene is slightly long. I agree it introduces a forward-pointing thread (Webb, AI safety), but this is a feature for readers of the shared universe and a minor confusion for standalone readers. The final image (ordinary birds, watched too long) is exactly right.

## Thematic Coherence

### Primary Theme: The Gap Between Measurement and Meaning
Mara measures everything. She cannot interpret anything. The gap between her extraordinary precision and her inability to understand what her instruments serve is the story's engine. This theme is introduced in Scene 1 (counting turbines, filing raven behavior), escalated through Movement II (measuring anomalies she cannot explain), and brought to crisis in Movement III (the data is "beautiful" -- a word that is not a measurement).

**Assessment**: Thematically coherent throughout. The theme never wavers, is never explicitly stated, and is consistently embodied through Mara's actions.

### Secondary Theme: Institutional Patience
The 1847 drill marks anchor a meditation on commitment across generations. Someone dug for thirty years without knowing what they would find. Mara builds for two years without knowing what she is building. The Order has been at this for seven hundred years. The theme is developed through three appearances of the drill marks (discovery, caretaking, farewell) and the cathedral metaphor (a space built for instruments that didn't yet exist).

**Assessment**: Effectively developed. The drill marks carry this theme without the story ever needing to state it.

## The Epilogue Question

The prior review noted the epilogue "opens a thread rather than closing." The structure-level assessment:

The coda serves three functions:
1. **Temporal scope**: Fifteen years compressed establishes the permanence of Mara's marking.
2. **Connection to novel timeline**: The article about Webb links The Cathedral to the parent novel's events.
3. **Final image**: Ordinary birds watched too long.

Function 1 is essential. Function 3 is perfect. Function 2 is the structural question. Does the article scene serve the story or the franchise?

**Assessment**: For the shared universe, Function 2 is valuable -- it places The Cathedral in the timeline and gives the reader who knows Webb a moment of recognition. For a standalone reading, the article is a loose thread. The story could end at "She had not mentioned the dreams to anyone" (line 359), skip to the birds on the power line (line 367), and be structurally complete. But the article passage adds only ~100 words and does not damage the structure. MEDIUM finding at most.

## Issue Register

### MEDIUM

**S-M1: Raven measurement section as scene vs. summary**
- **Location**: Lines 150-163
- **Problem**: The pacing shifts from dramatized scene to research summary. This breaks the otherwise consistent narrative mode.
- **Suggestion**: Rewrite as a physical scene (Mara on the bench, counting, graphing). The material is correct; the rendering needs to be embodied.

### LOW

**S-L1: Bird-to-headache transition**
- **Location**: Lines 163-167
- **Problem**: The July-to-September gap is handled by a scene break but the shift from external to internal anomaly could be smoother.
- **Suggestion**: Consider a single transitional sentence after the scene break that grounds the new month physically before introducing the headaches.

**S-L2: Coda article as loose thread**
- **Location**: Lines 361-365
- **Problem**: The article about an AI safety researcher introduces forward momentum in what should be a closing movement.
- **Suggestion**: If standalone reading is a priority, compress the article passage. If shared-universe reading is the target, keep as is.
