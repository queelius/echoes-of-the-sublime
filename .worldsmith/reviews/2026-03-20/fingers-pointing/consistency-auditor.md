## Canonical Alignment

### Medieval Order Functions (lore.md)

The lore specifies three primary functions for the medieval Order (~1326-1714):

1. **Caring for casualties** -- housing the "God-touched." The story dramatizes this: Ensho is fed rice gruel, his robe is changed, the monks sweep around him. Wuzhun explicitly models this: "We house them. We feed them. We observe." **Aligned.**

2. **Preserving reports** -- recording what practitioners perceive. Soken writes letters (first coded, then factual). Three replies arrive documenting similar cases across traditions. **Aligned.**

3. **Connecting traditions** -- practitioners recognizing each other across frameworks. Wuzhun (Chan), Dorje Wangchuk (Vajrayana), Kaido (Shugendo) converge. "Different languages, different frameworks, same underlying territory." **Aligned.**

### Capture Mechanism (worldbuilding.md)

The capture mechanism operates identically to the modern version:

- **Recursive self-perception**: "The hearing is the heard. The hearing of the hearing." This is consciousness perceiving consciousness, matching the "no base case" structure. **Aligned.**
- **Through contemplation, not computation**: Ensho achieves this through "thirty years of patience and a koan that would not resolve." The slow-capture pathway is consistent with the lore's assertion that "the contemplative path was slower, not safer." **Aligned.**
- **The elastic limit**: "Each one a fraction deeper, each one stretching the membrane between perception and the thing perceived until the membrane was no longer there." Matches worldbuilding.md's elastic limit metaphor. **Aligned.**
- **Irremediability**: "He did not come back" -- permanent, no recovery. The retrieved monk from Echizen who "never sat zazen again" with "ordinary" eyes afterward also matches the capture spectrum's implication that intervention costs. **Aligned.**

### Information Hazard (lore.md, worldbuilding.md)

The coded letter is a pre-computational information hazard containment measure. Soken's reasoning -- "certain descriptions of certain states could produce those states in a mind prepared to receive them" -- directly matches the novel's information hazard taxonomy, specifically the "perceptual hazard" category (worldbuilding.md). The story correctly avoids the formal taxonomy vocabulary while dramatizing the same principle. **Aligned.**

### Chronological Constraints

- No European practitioners appear -- correct for 1340s Japan. The lore specifies "The connection between European and East Asian networks does not yet exist" for this period. **Aligned.**
- No formal Order structure -- the network operates through "accumulated trust" and "shared recognition," not institutions. Consistent with the pre-Leibniz era. **Aligned.**
- Tofukuji (Kyoto) is referenced as Jisho's temple -- historically valid (established 1236). **Aligned.**
- Wuzhun visiting Gozan temples in Kamakura -- plausible for 1340s Chinese-Japanese Zen exchange. **Aligned.**

## Findings

### C-M1: Age Arithmetic Ambiguity (MEDIUM, confidence: moderate)

**Location**: Section I, lines 8-22

The story says:
- Line 8: "unremarkable for thirty years"
- Line 12: "arrived at fourteen" and "thirty years of practice"
- Line 14: koan assigned at nineteen
- Line 16: "Thirty years with the same question"
- Line 22: "Ensho's forty-third year" (when the change begins)

If Ensho arrived at 14, "thirty years of practice" puts him at 44. But the change begins in his "forty-third year" (line 22), which precedes the crisis by "two years" (line 26), placing the crisis at ~45.

The "thirty years with the same question" (line 16) is more problematic: if the koan was assigned at 19, thirty years puts him at 49. But the change is first noticed at 43. This means the "thirty years" in line 16 describes a future state -- Soken's retrospective summary from later -- rather than the moment the change begins.

This is not wrong per se (the narrator is retrospective), but the temporal framing telescopes between "thirty years of practice," "thirty years with the same question," and "forty-third year" in a way that could confuse attentive readers. The discrepancy is ~5-7 years depending on reading.

**Suggestion**: Change "Thirty years with the same question" (line 16) to "More than twenty years with the same question," or adjust "forty-third year" (line 22) upward to close the gap.

### C-L1: Wuzhun Arrival Sequence (LOW, confidence: low)

**Location**: Section II, lines 149-167

Soken sends the letter to Jisho at Tofukuji in Kyoto. Wuzhun, in Kamakura (further from a northern mountain temple), arrives first. Dorje Wangchuk, already in Kyoto (where the letter was sent), arrives three days later. This is slightly counterintuitive geographically.

The text partially addresses this: Wuzhun heard "through the network of connections" (not necessarily through Soken's letter directly), and the arrivals happen "over the following weeks" (vague enough to accommodate variable relay times). Not a factual error, but the reader may briefly wonder why the person in Kyoto didn't arrive first.

**Suggestion**: No change required. If desired, a half-sentence noting that Wuzhun happened to be traveling northward when word reached him would resolve any ambiguity.

### C-L2: No Explicit Date in Manuscript (LOW, observation)

The manuscript contains no date, year, or century reference. This is appropriate for the historical register -- a 14th-century Zen monk would not think in Western calendar terms. The 1340s setting is established only in the lore docs. **This is a strength, not a problem.**
