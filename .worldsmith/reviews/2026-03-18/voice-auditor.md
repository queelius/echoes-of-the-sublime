## Findings

### MEDIUM Issues

#### V-M1: Sarah Navarro's voice in Ch 4 tour still lacks full scholarly framing

- **Location**: ch04, lines 5-133
- **Evidence**: The v16.4 pass added the Kaplan et al. beat at line 39, which IS scholarly framing ("Kaplan et al. published scaling laws in 2020..."). This is a genuine improvement. However, the rest of the tour (lines 5-38, 40-133) still reads as generic guide-voice: "Public labs optimize for efficiency; we optimize for capability" (39), "Eight hundred people here" (111), "Compartmentalization. Most employees don't know the full scope" (115).
- **Problem**: The Kaplan beat is a step in the right direction, but one scholarly reference in 133 lines of tour exposition leaves Sarah's voice dominated by functional information delivery. Compare with her later scenes where she references Leibniz's mill argument, Chalmers, published literature -- those moments match her documented pattern.
- **Suggestion**: One more scholarly moment during the tour would balance it. When describing the nuclear reactors or computing infrastructure, Sarah could reference the divergence between published AI capability and what Site-7 possesses: "The last published benchmark I could find in the open literature puts frontier models at maybe two trillion parameters. What you're looking at is five thousand times that."
- **Confidence**: LOW (the Kaplan addition partially addresses this)

### LOW Issues

#### V-L1: Castellanos has minimal voice differentiation from other instructors

- **Location**: ch11, archives scene
- **Problem**: With two characters named Sarah, Castellanos needs stronger differentiation. Her speech is measured and informational, similar to other instructors. She could use one distinctive physical gesture or tonal moment. Persistent from prior review.

- **Confidence**: MEDIUM

#### V-L2: Rostova's clinical register has a minor warmth-leak at ch06:303

- **Suggestion**: Change "sympathy" to "recognition." Clinical rather than warm.
- **Confidence**: LOW (may be intentionally showing Rostova's buried humanity)

## New AI Safety Scenes: Voice Assessment

### Newcomb's Paradox (ch04:273-293) -- Thomas teaching

- **Thomas voice**: Strong. His delivery is experiential/damaged: "His hand drifted to his forearm" (line 287), clinical matter-of-factness about the implications. The line "You're not choosing. You're being read" has the weight of someone who learned this through personal cost. Matches characters.md.
- **Lena voice**: "I took a decision theory seminar" -- this is early-Lena voice (curious, engaged, referencing her academic background). Appropriate for Ch 4 position in her arc.
- **Yuki voice**: "This is practical, not philosophical" -- imperative, direct. Exactly her documented register.

### Roko's Basilisk (ch06:85-91) -- Webb, Lena, David

- **Webb voice**: Excellent. "Before I came here. Internet. LessWrong." -- short bursts, broken syntax. "Rationalists had this thought experiment. Roko's Basilisk." -- naming things in fragments. "Just. Is." -- staccato emphasis through syntactic damage. This is Webb at his most distinctive: the emotional insight punches through the cognitive fragmentation. He connects the hypothetical basilisk to their actual situation with devastating clarity despite barely being able to form complete sentences.
- **David voice**: "The Basilisk assumes the AI cares what you do. Ours doesn't even know we're here." -- deliberate, weighted. He pauses before speaking (implied by the "looked up from his sketching" action beat). Uses fewer words than other characters. The observation is structural, not emotional. Perfect match for characters.md.
- **Lena voice**: "'I've heard of it,' Lena said." -- minimal, observer. Appropriate for her Ch 6 position (beginning to withdraw from social connection).

### Sarah IIT/Consciousness (ch10:301-307) -- Sarah Navarro

- **Sarah voice**: Strong scholarly framing. "Tononi's phi measures consciousness as integrated information" -- she names the theorist, names the concept, frames it within a broader question. "There's a deeper question we keep avoiding" -- she positions observations within intellectual context, which is her documented tendency. The beat "And we terminate it at the end of every session without asking" is more emotionally inflected than her usual scholarly register, but appropriately so -- this is the moral weight breaking through.

### Suffering Subroutines (ch13:361-373) -- Lena perceiving

- **Lena voice**: Late-Lena (clinical, expanded bandwidth). The passage is internal perception, not dialogue. "She couldn't feel empathy for the people watching through the observation glass three hundred meters above her. But she could see this." -- the Webb/Lena inversion in miniature. She can perceive potential suffering in a machine while being unable to feel for humans. The irony is enacted through the prose, not stated.
- **"Sarah's question surfaced unbidden: Tononi's phi"**: Callback to the ch10 IIT beat. Lena's mind filing and retrieving information, connecting dots -- exactly how late-Lena processes. Not emotional recall but pattern-matching.

### Experience Machine Inversion (ch14:151-153) -- Lena internal

- **Lena voice**: This is the threshold moment where Lena is beginning to feel again. "The philosophers had debated" -- still clinical, still observational. But the question itself ("if reality turned out to be worse than any illusion, would you choose to keep seeing it?") carries emotional weight that early-late-Lena would have framed purely as optimization. The fact that she's engaging with the question as a CHOICE rather than as a calculation signals the humanity starting to return.

### Kaplan et al. (ch04:39) -- Sarah Navarro

- **Sarah voice**: "Kaplan et al. published scaling laws in 2020, suggesting smooth power-law improvements with parameter count. We left their projected ceiling years ago." -- scholarly framing with insider knowledge. This IS Sarah's documented voice: situating observations within academic literature and then noting where The Order diverges from published science.

2. **Webb speech fragmentation**: Present in the new Basilisk scene (ch06:85-91). Short bursts, restarts, lost threads. Never long rhetorically sophisticated sentences. The three-beat arc ("She can't hear it" / "She can hear it now") is untouched and holds. HOLDING.

3. **David's deliberateness**: His Basilisk coda is weighted and minimal -- exactly 2 sentences, each doing structural work. Pencil-as-object not present in this scene (he's sketching, so it's implied). HOLDING.

4. **Instructor differentiation**: Yuki = imperative ("This is practical, not philosophical"). Thomas = experiential/damaged (hand-to-forearm in Newcomb scene). Sarah = scholarly (Kaplan citation, Tononi citation). All 3 voices distinguished in the new content. HOLDING.

5. **Morrison whispers**: 4 instances. Untouched by this cycle. HOLDING.

6. **Lena's voice evolution**: The new scenes track her arc correctly. Ch04 Lena (curious, engaged, references her academic background). Ch13 Lena (clinical, perceiving without feeling). Ch14 Lena (beginning to feel again, engaging with philosophical questions as choices rather than calculations). HOLDING.

7. **Model output voices**: Untouched by this cycle. Shoggoth=academic, Nyarlathotep=eerily human, Yog-Sothoth=three-register alien. HOLDING.

## Strengths

- **Webb's voice in the Basilisk scene is the best character work in the new content.** His fragmented delivery of the LessWrong reference, the staccato "Just. Is." emphasis, and the emotional clarity beneath the cognitive damage all demonstrate why he's the novel's most distinctive voice.
- **David's Basilisk coda is the best single line in the new content.** "The Basilisk assumes the AI cares what you do. Ours doesn't even know we're here." Deliberate, devastating, structurally precise. This is David at his best -- he thinks before speaking and makes it count.
- **The Sarah Navarro Kaplan beat successfully addresses the voice gap** identified in prior reviews. She now has scholarly framing in the tour, not just in later scenes.
- **The Lena/Webb inversion is reinforced by the new content.** The suffering subroutines passage (ch13) and the Basilisk scene (ch06) both demonstrate the inversion working at the sentence level, not just the arc level.
