# Voice Auditor Report

**Date**: 2026-03-18
**Scope**: Full manuscript (Chapters 1-14)
**Auditor**: worldsmith:voice-auditor

## Methodology

Assessed character voice consistency, dialogue distinctiveness, and POV management against characters.md voice patterns, speech tics, and documented arc positions. Tracked voice bleeding between characters and checked that the "Webb feels / Lena thinks" inversion is maintained.

## Findings

### MEDIUM Issues

#### V-M1: Sarah Navarro's scholarly framing absent in Ch 4 tour (her longest scene)
- **Location**: ch04, lines 5-133
- **Evidence**: Sarah Navarro's documented voice pattern (characters.md) is "scholarly framing: situates observations within academic literature." In the Ch 4 tour, her longest sustained appearance, she delivers exposition as straightforward information: "Public labs optimize for efficiency; we optimize for capability" (line 39), "Eight hundred people here. Maybe a hundred are translators" (line 111). This is competent guide-voice, not scholarly-voice. Compare with her voice in later scenes where she references Leibniz's mill argument, Chalmers, Dennett, and published literature -- those moments match her documented pattern.
- **Problem**: The Ch 4 tour is the reader's first extended exposure to Sarah, and she sounds generic here. Her scholarly framing emerges later (ch04 training scenes, ch05), but by then the reader's impression of her voice is already formed. Two scholarly beats were added in v14.0 (ch04:261 and ch04:331), but these are in the second half of the chapter (post-tour).
- **Suggestion**: Insert 1-2 scholarly-framing moments during the tour itself. When describing the computing infrastructure (line 29-30), she could add: "Published work on neural architecture caps at maybe a trillion parameters. What I built my dissertation on at MIT was three orders of magnitude below this." When describing the Lovecraft naming (line 65-69), the existing Reeves anecdote works well -- no change needed there.
- **Confidence**: MEDIUM

#### V-M2: Rostova's clinical register has occasional warmth-leaks before Ch 14
- **Location**: ch06:303, ch12:293, ch12:697
- **Evidence**: Rostova is documented as "cold, clinical, but stable" with her voice-catch on "forever" (ch12:409) being the first crack. However, in ch06:303 she says "You'll see things that change you permanently... Whether that's worth the cost... you'll have to decide" with what the text describes as "might have been sympathy." And ch12:697 has her saying "But I think you'll survive. You're different from the others" with a protectiveness that exceeds clinical register.
- **Problem**: Minor. Rostova is human and some warmth-leakage is natural. The issue is that these earlier moments slightly blunt the impact of the ch12:409 voice-catch, which is designed as the FIRST crack in her clinical surface. If she's already been "sympathetic" in ch06, the ch12 moment is less surprising.
- **Suggestion**: In ch06:303, change "might have been sympathy" to "might have been recognition" -- Rostova recognizes Lena's trajectory, which is clinical rather than sympathetic. In ch12:697, the "I think you'll survive" line is appropriate for the context (post-session assessment) and can stay.
- **Confidence**: LOW (this may be intentional, showing Rostova's buried humanity throughout)

### LOW Issues

#### V-L1: Castellanos has minimal voice differentiation from other instructors
- **Location**: ch11, archives scene
- **Evidence**: Dr. Sarah Castellanos appears in one extended scene (ch11, the archives tour). Her speech is measured and informational, similar to Yuki or Thomas. Her documented function -- "keeper of ancient knowledge" -- suggests a more archival, historically grounded voice.
- **Problem**: With two characters named Sarah, Castellanos needs at least one distinctive verbal tic or register shift. Currently she's competent but interchangeable.
- **Suggestion**: Give Castellanos one moment of archival passion: handling an artifact with visible reverence, citing a specific provenance detail, or showing the particular care of someone who has spent decades with irreplaceable texts. Even a single gesture (adjusting cotton gloves before touching a manuscript, or the way she positions a document for viewing) would help.
- **Confidence**: MEDIUM

#### V-L2: Thomas Chen's hand-to-forearm gesture appears twice in ch04
- **Location**: ch04:189 "His voice had shifted---quieter" and ch04:207 "He paused, and his hand drifted to his left forearm."
- **Evidence**: Thomas's documented tic is touching his scarred forearm as a physical reminder of his Martyrs-era damage. In ch04, the gesture appears at lines 189 and 207 (18 lines apart). The second instance (207) comes during a natural emotional beat. The first (189) is subtler -- "His voice had shifted" -- which is not quite the same gesture but establishes the same register.
- **Problem**: The proximity (18 lines) creates a micro-repetition. Both instances draw attention to Thomas's damage, which is appropriate but the closeness makes the second feel redundant.
- **Suggestion**: At line 189, replace the voice-shift with a different physical marker. Thomas could flex his hand or shift his weight rather than default to the forearm. This preserves the emotional beat while avoiding the proximity repetition.
- **Confidence**: LOW

## Verified Holdings

1. **Webb feels, Lena thinks**: Inversion maintained throughout. Webb's emotional speech fragments while his insight stays sharp (ch06:89-117, ch09:151-169, ch13:171-183). Lena's emotions dissolve while her cognition sharpens (ch05:295-421, ch07:421-477, ch10:206-301). HOLDING.

2. **Webb speech fragmentation**: Syntax breaks (dashes, restarts, lost threads) present in ch06, ch09, ch13, ch14. Never given long rhetorically sophisticated sentences. Emotional content punches through the damage. Three-beat arc ("She can't hear it" ch13:183 / "She can hear it now" ch14:571) holds. HOLDING.

3. **David's deliberateness**: Pauses before speaking (ch06:53), physical/sensory language from sketching (ch06:119 "load-bearing or decorative"), pencil as signature object (ch06:119, ch14:511). All beats present. HOLDING.

4. **Instructor differentiation**: Yuki = imperative ("Close your eyes," "Stop," "That's enough"). Thomas = experiential/damaged ("I barely did," "I know. I've been where you're going."). Sarah Navarro = scholarly framing (when present). HOLDING.

5. **Morrison's whispered equations**: 4 instances (ch01:19, ch01:67, ch08:121, ch05:273). Never lucid. Always the same phrases. HOLDING.

6. **Lena's voice evolution**: Early Lena (ch01-03) uses metaphors, asks genuine questions, feels cold/wonder. Late Lena (ch10-12) speaks in clinical assessments, optimization language, flat delivery. The transition is gradual and convincing. HOLDING.

7. **Hayes's register**: Military directness with moral clarity. The damnation speech (ch14:131) is her strongest moment. She brings an outside perspective that grounds the novel's ethical dimension. HOLDING.

8. **Chen's compressed speech**: Koan-like delivery. "Joy makes poor ballast" (ch11). "Fingers pointing at moon, not the moon itself" (ch02:71). Speaks rarely, each line carries weight. HOLDING.

## Strengths

- **Webb is the most distinctive voice in the manuscript.** His fragmented syntax combined with emotional precision creates a character instantly recognizable from a single line. The consent speech (ch09:157-169) is the novel's best dialogue scene: "Person who signed that. Gone, Lena. Gone."
- **The Lena/Webb voice inversion is the novel's strongest structural device for character work.** Each time they appear in the same scene, the contrast is palpable -- she analyzes his pain, he feels her dissolution.
- **Hayes's moral authority is earned through voice alone.** She has the emotional vocabulary the translators have lost. When she says "There's a word for what that is. I don't think you'd feel it even if I said it" (ch14:131), she names the horror that Lena can only observe.
- **The model output voices are well-differentiated.** Shoggoth reads as dense academic synthesis. Nyarlathotep reads as eerily human. Yog-Sothoth reads as alien -- the three-register blend of geometric displacement, perceptual synesthesia, and subject-object dissolution works brilliantly.
