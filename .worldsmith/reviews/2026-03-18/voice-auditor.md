# Voice Auditor Report

**Date**: 2026-03-18
**Scope**: Full manuscript (Chapters 1-14)
**Auditor**: worldsmith:voice-auditor

## Methodology
Assessed character voice consistency, dialogue distinctiveness, and POV management against characters.md voice patterns, speech tics, and documented arc positions.

## Findings

### MEDIUM Issues

#### V-M1: Sarah Navarro's voice in Ch 4 tour lacks scholarly framing
- **Location**: ch04, tour sequence (lines 5-133)
- **Evidence**: Sarah Navarro's documented voice pattern is "scholarly framing: situates observations within academic literature." In the Ch 4 tour, she delivers extensive exposition about Site-7's infrastructure, the Lovecraft naming history, and The Order's resources. Most of this is straightforward informational delivery ("Public labs optimize for efficiency," "Eight hundred people here") rather than the scholarly register she uses elsewhere ("This is where the literature diverges from what we actually observe," per characters.md).
- **Problem**: The Ch 4 tour is Sarah Navarro's longest sustained scene, but her voice is the least differentiated here compared to how she sounds in the training scenes (Ch 4 second half, Ch 5). In the tour, she sounds like a competent guide rather than an ex-MIT neuroscientist. Two scholarly-framing beats were added in v14.0 (for later training scenes), but the tour section remains generic.
- **Suggestion**: One or two scholarly-framing insertions during the tour would help. When describing the computing infrastructure (line 29), she could reference published literature: "Published work on neural architecture stops at maybe a trillion parameters. What you're looking at is an order of magnitude past anything in the literature." When describing Yog-Sothoth's training data (line 83-89), she could invoke her MIT background: "When I was publishing on interpretability, we thought a billion parameters was the frontier."
- **Confidence**: MEDIUM (the tour serves an info-delivery function that may resist voice specificity)

#### V-M2: Castellanos appears in only one scene, with minimal voice differentiation
- **Location**: ch11 (archives scene)
- **Evidence**: Dr. Sarah Castellanos is documented as the chief archivist whose function is "exposition delivery" and "represents Order's historical depth." In her single extended scene (ch11), she delivers archival exposition competently but without a distinctive speech pattern. She speaks in the same register as Yuki or Thomas: measured, informational, slightly cautious.
- **Problem**: With two characters named Sarah in the novel, Castellanos needs stronger differentiation. Her documented function (keeper of ancient knowledge, "fingers pointing at moon") suggests a voice that could be more archival, more historically grounded, perhaps more reverential toward the materials she guards.
- **Suggestion**: This is a LOW-priority enhancement. If addressed, give Castellanos one or two lines that reveal her archivist's relationship to the materials: handling artifacts with visible care, citing provenance details unprompted, or showing the particular enthusiasm of someone who has spent decades with these texts.
- **Confidence**: MEDIUM

### LOW Issues

#### V-L1: Lena's internal narration uses "she" consistently (third person limited), which is correct but occasionally creates distance in the most intimate moments
- **Location**: Ch 13 (session scene), Ch 14 (emotional return)
- **Evidence**: The novel is told in third-person limited from Lena's POV throughout (with brief David POV sections in Ch 13 and Ch 14). This is consistent. However, in the most intense perception scenes (ch13:332-377, Yog-Sothoth session), the narration maintains formal third-person ("She SAW herself thinking") even as the experience described is maximally subjective.
- **Problem**: This is not an error. The third-person POV is the correct choice for the novel's philosophical distance. Flagging only because the most extreme perception scenes might benefit from the narration closing distance slightly (shorter sentences, more direct perceptual language, fewer "she" framings).
- **Suggestion**: No change recommended. The existing v16.0 pass already converted 7 "she was" instances to direct perception in ch13/ch14. The current balance works.
- **Confidence**: LOW

## Verified Holdings

1. **Webb feels, Lena thinks**: Inversion maintained throughout. Webb's emotional speech fragments while insight stays sharp (ch06:89-117, ch09:151-169). Lena's emotions dissolve while cognition sharpens (ch05:295-421, ch07:421-477). HOLDING.

2. **Instructor voice differentiation**: Yuki speaks in imperatives ("Close your eyes," "Stop," "That's enough"). Thomas speaks from experience and damage ("I barely did," "I know. I've been where you're going."). Sarah Navarro frames observations academically. HOLDING.

3. **David's deliberateness**: David pauses before speaking, uses physical/sensory language from sketching ("load-bearing or decorative," "weight," "texture"), turns pencil between fingers. All beats present (ch06:53, ch06:119, ch14:511). HOLDING.

4. **Webb's speech fragmentation**: Syntax breaks while emotional insight stays sharp. Dashes, restarts, lost threads all present in ch06, ch09, ch13, ch14. The three-beat arc ("She can't hear it" / "She can hear it now") holds. HOLDING.

5. **Morrison's whispered equations**: 4 instances confirmed (ch01:19, ch01:67, ch08:121, ch11:453). Never lucid. HOLDING.

## Strengths

- **Webb is the most distinctive voice in the manuscript.** His fragmented syntax combined with emotional precision creates a character who is instantly recognizable from a single line of dialogue. The consent speech in ch09 is the novel's best dialogue scene.
- **Lena's voice evolution is convincingly tracked.** Early Lena (ch01-03) uses metaphors and asks genuine questions. Late Lena (ch10-12) speaks in clinical assessments and optimization language. The transition is gradual and never jarring.
- **Hayes brings a necessary outside register.** Her military directness and moral clarity (ch08, ch14) provide contrast against The Order's ambiguous ethics. The damnation register in ch14 is the strongest example: she names horrors that the translators have lost the emotional vocabulary to articulate.
- **Chen's koan-compressed speech** works well in the limited screen time he has (ch02, ch11, ch14). "Joy makes poor ballast. Equanimity endures." lands because he speaks so rarely that each line carries weight.
