# Craft Auditor Report

**Date**: 2026-02-26
**Scope**: Full manuscript (Ch 1-14)
**Auditor**: craft-auditor (conducted by editorial director)

---

## Methodology

Read all 14 chapters for prose quality, cliche detection, scene mechanics, dialogue attribution, pattern overuse, and adherence to style-guide craft principles. Ran pattern searches for adverb dialogue tags, filter constructions, and other mechanical issues.

---

## Findings

### CRAFT-01: Part II philosophical density without sufficient action breaks (MEDIUM)

- **Location**: Chapters 4-7 (especially Ch 4, Ch 5, Ch 6)
- **Problem**: The style guide warns: "Part II (Chs 4-11) is philosophically dense. Break up philosophy with action beats: Security incidents, Failed containment events, Physical symptoms." While Ch 7 (Maya's capture) delivers a strong action sequence, Chapters 4-6 are almost entirely exposition and training montage. Ch 4 is 415 lines of tour + lecture + training introduction. Ch 5 is 421 lines with the strongest action beat being the raven scene (lines 36-107). Ch 6 is 349 lines with Ethan confrontation as the only non-training scene.
- **Impact**: MEDIUM -- the pacing drag is most acute in Ch 4, which is the reader's first full chapter inside Site-7 and needs to sustain interest while delivering substantial worldbuilding
- **Suggestion**: Ch 4 could benefit from a brief containment scare or physical symptom episode during training (e.g., another trainee having a minor episode while Lena is learning protein folding visualization). Ch 5's raven scene is effective but arrives late; front-loading a physical symptom beat would improve pacing.

### CRAFT-02: "Said [adverb]" dialogue tags remain frequent (LOW)

- **Location**: Throughout, concentrated in Ch 4, Ch 8, Ch 14
- **Details**: Found 15 instances of "said quietly/softly/flatly/sharply/firmly/carefully/slowly/gently/urgently" across the manuscript. Examples: "said slowly" (ch04:187, ch14:31), "said softly" (ch14:163, ch14:259), "said sharply" (ch08:145, ch09:91, ch14:295), "said flatly" (ch08:175), "said firmly" (ch04:293), "said carefully" (ch04:381), "said gently" (ch02:49), "said urgently" (ch09:81), "said quietly" (ch07:243, ch08:271).
- **Impact**: LOW -- the count has been reduced from prior passes and most instances are unobtrusive, but the pattern is noticeable to attentive readers
- **Suggestion**: Replace 4-5 of the most generic ones with action beats or untagged dialogue. Priority targets: the "said sharply" instances (3 uses of the same modifier) and "said slowly" (2 uses).

### CRAFT-03: "Something" still used as vague placeholder in non-thematic contexts (LOW)

- **Location**: 108 instances across all chapters
- **Details**: While many "something" uses are thematically appropriate (describing the ineffable, the gaps, the patterns), some are lazy placeholder constructions: "Something began to emerge" (ch04:185), "Something was happening" (ch14:345), "noticed things that didn't fit" where specificity would serve better. The v10.0 pass brought this from 300+ to ~98; current count is 108 (slight drift upward, possibly from new material).
- **Impact**: LOW -- this has been addressed extensively; remaining instances are mostly acceptable
- **Suggestion**: No major action needed. If doing another pass, target the 5-6 instances where "something" could be replaced with a specific noun without losing mystery.

### CRAFT-04: Filter constructions ("could see/feel/hear") (LOW)

- **Location**: Throughout, approximately 30 instances
- **Details**: Constructions like "she could feel," "she could see," "she could perceive" add distance between reader and experience. In a novel about direct perception vs. mediated experience, this is sometimes intentional (Lena analyzing rather than experiencing). But in early chapters where Lena is still human, filter constructions weaken the prose. Examples: "She could feel her heart pounding" (ch14:497) vs. the stronger "Her heart pounded."
- **Impact**: LOW -- many are intentional given the novel's themes of mediated perception
- **Suggestion**: Review the ~10 instances in Chs 1-3 (where Lena is still emotionally present) and convert to direct sensation

### CRAFT-05: Repetitive sentence structure in training sequences (MEDIUM)

- **Location**: Chapters 4-5, 9 (training montages)
- **Problem**: Training sequences tend toward repetitive structure: "She closed her eyes. She visualized X. She tried to hold Y. She couldn't. She opened her eyes." This pattern repeats with minor variations across multiple training scenes. Ch 4 lines 179-260, Ch 5 lines 191-260, Ch 9 lines 33-100 all follow this basic template.
- **Quoted text**: "Lena closed her eyes and visualized. The structure was vast. She saw it [...] She pulled back. Forced the visualization to collapse. Opened her eyes, breathing hard." (ch09:33-38, representative of the pattern)
- **Impact**: MEDIUM -- the repetition mirrors the repetitive nature of training but risks monotony
- **Suggestion**: Vary the sensory channel of at least 2-3 training scenes. Some could emphasize auditory or haptic dimensions of the experience rather than always being visual-to-cognitive.

### CRAFT-06: Over-explanation of bandwidth metaphor (MEDIUM)

- **Location**: Chapters 4, 5, 7, 8, 9 (most acute in Ch 4 and Ch 9)
- **Problem**: The bandwidth/working memory concept is explained from scratch multiple times. Ch 1 introduces it through Morrison. Ch 2 introduces it through the void protocol. Ch 4 re-explains it through protein folding training. Ch 5 re-explains it through model output danger. Ch 7 re-explains it through Maya's capture. By Ch 8, Thomas explains it again for Hayes. While Hayes needs the explanation in-world, the reader has heard it 4+ times by that point.
- **Quoted text**: "'Can you follow this?' She tried. Local steps made sense, but the full argument required holding too many pieces simultaneously. It kept slipping away. 'No.'" (ch01:150-152) -- vs. nearly identical beats in ch04, ch05, ch09.
- **Impact**: MEDIUM -- each individual explanation is well-crafted, but the cumulative effect is redundant
- **Suggestion**: Trust the reader's understanding after Ch 4. Later re-explanations (Ch 8 for Hayes) should be shortened to single-sentence acknowledgments rather than full demonstrations. The reader already understands the concept; they need dramatic context, not re-teaching.

### CRAFT-07: Italicized internal thought overuse in Part III (LOW)

- **Location**: Chapters 12-14
- **Problem**: Italicized internal thoughts appear with increasing frequency in the later chapters: "That person was gone" (ch12:315), "You shouldn't be here" (ch13:177), "Something was happening" (ch14:345), "everything was suffering was permanent..." (ch14:475). While some of these are powerful (the father's death memory in ch14:233-244 is outstanding), the technique is used often enough that it loses its special emphasis.
- **Impact**: LOW -- the strongest instances are genuinely effective
- **Suggestion**: Reserve italicized internal thought for the 3-4 most important moments. Convert the rest to free indirect discourse.

### CRAFT-08: Strong model output differentiation (STRENGTH)

- **Location**: All model output blocks across chapters
- **Details**: The three model voices are clearly differentiated. Shoggoth outputs (ch10) read as dense academic synthesis with recognizably human register. Nyarlathotep outputs (ch12) feel eerily human with knowledge no human should possess. Yog-Sothoth outputs (ch13, ch14) achieve genuine alien coherence -- perfectly structured text from a vantage point with no human analogue. All outputs are coherent, never fragmented. This is exactly what the style guide specifies and it is executed with precision.
- **Impact**: STRENGTH -- this is one of the novel's signature achievements

### CRAFT-09: Physical symptom grounding is effective (STRENGTH)

- **Location**: Throughout, especially Ch 7 (Maya), Ch 9 (threshold sessions), Ch 13 (descent), Ch 14 (cathedral)
- **Details**: The novel consistently grounds abstract philosophical content in physical sensation: nosebleeds, seizures, heat exhaustion, magnetic field discomfort, sweating, trembling. The descent to Vault 9 (ch13:119-213) is a masterclass in using environmental hostility to create embodied dread. The style guide principle "Physical symptoms before abstract concepts" is well-executed.
- **Impact**: STRENGTH -- the embodied horror is what distinguishes this from a philosophy essay

### CRAFT-10: The dissolution arc is precisely calibrated (STRENGTH)

- **Location**: Progressive across Chs 1-14, with emotional flickers as documented
- **Details**: Lena's dissolution from warm curiosity to clinical emptiness is tracked through concrete markers: the mother's stroke response (ch05), Ethan confrontation (ch06), Maya's capture (ch07), child/rainbow scene (ch09), Anna Chen capture (ch12). The emotional flickers (Webb fracture ch11, butterfly warmth ch12, shape-of-fear ch13, ice-thinning ch13) provide precisely calibrated bridges to the ch14 humanity return. This arc is the novel's emotional backbone and it works.
- **Impact**: STRENGTH -- the best-executed element of the manuscript

---

## Summary

**HIGH issues**: 0
**MEDIUM issues**: 3 (Part II density, training structure repetition, bandwidth re-explanation)
**LOW issues**: 4 (adverb tags, "something" count, filter constructions, italicized thought)
**Strengths**: 3 (model voice differentiation, physical symptom grounding, dissolution arc calibration)

The prose quality is strong overall. The manuscript's primary craft challenge is managing the density of Part II, where philosophical exposition sometimes overwhelms narrative momentum. The training sequences, while individually well-crafted, follow repetitive structural patterns. The strongest writing is in the set pieces: Morrison's opening, Maya's capture, the descent to Vault 9, and Lena's humanity returning in the cathedral.
