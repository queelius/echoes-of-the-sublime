# Voice Auditor Report

**Date**: 2026-03-14 (v2)
**Scope**: Full manuscript, Chapters 1-14

## Methodology

Read all dialogue and free indirect discourse for character voice consistency, distinctiveness, and POV discipline. Checked against character docs for voice patterns, speech tics, and arc positions. Focused on finding NEW issues not flagged in prior reviews.

## Findings

### No HIGH issues found.

### M1: Sarah Navarro and Sarah Castellanos blur in reader memory (MEDIUM)

- **Location**: Sarah Navarro appears in ch02, ch04, ch05, ch07, ch08, ch09. Sarah Castellanos appears in ch11.
- **Problem**: Two significant secondary characters share the first name "Sarah." Sarah Navarro is the early guide/instructor; Sarah Castellanos is the archivist. The manuscript uses bare "Sarah" in dialogue tags approximately 30 times. While the characters never appear in the same scene, the shared first name creates a low-level confusion for readers processing the novel's large cast. When ch11 introduces "Sarah Castellanos" and then refers to her as "Sarah" in dialogue tags, readers may momentarily conflate her with Sarah Navarro, especially during a first read where character names are still consolidating.
- **Suggestion**: Use "Castellanos" consistently in ch11 dialogue tags rather than bare "Sarah." This is already partially done (ch11:7 introduces her as "Dr. Sarah Castellanos" and several tags use "Castellanos"), but some tags revert to bare "Sarah." Alternatively, if a future pass is planned, rename Castellanos to avoid the collision entirely.

### M2: "We don't know" as institutional chorus (MEDIUM)

- **Location**: ch04:101, ch04:199, ch04:269, ch05:59, ch05:125, ch05:133, ch05:223, ch08:129, ch10:285, ch10:449, ch11:59, ch11:79, ch11:107, ch14:23
- **Problem**: 14 instances of "We don't know" as direct dialogue. This phrase is spoken by Sarah (3), Thomas (3), Yuki (2), Rostova (2), Castellanos (1), David (1), and the group (1). While thematically appropriate (The Order genuinely operates at the frontier of the unknown), the identical phrasing across all characters erases voice distinctiveness. Thomas should admit ignorance differently than Yuki, who should admit it differently than Rostova.
- **Suggestion**: Keep the ch05:125 "all three said again, that disturbing synchronization" moment -- it's intentional and effective. For 4-5 other instances, differentiate by voice: Thomas might say "I've been sitting with that question for three years" (experiential/damaged), Yuki might say "That's not a question we're equipped to answer" (imperative/direct), Sarah might say "The literature is silent on this" (scholarly).
- **Cross-ref**: Also flagged by consistency-auditor.

### L1: Webb's speech fragmentation is inconsistent in ch06 (LOW)

- **Location**: ch06:89-117
- **Problem**: Webb's speech in ch06 mixes fragmented and fluent registers. Lines 89-94 are appropriately fragmented ("Patterns were real." "Not hallucinations."). But lines 96-98 and 105-107 are syntactically sophisticated multi-clause sentences that contradict his documented speech pattern of collapsing clauses and lost threads. The v12.0 pass fragmented his ch09 and ch14 speeches but ch06 was not fully addressed.
- **Suggestion**: Low priority. Webb is earlier in his deterioration arc in ch06 (before his Yog-Sothoth session), so more fluent speech is somewhat justified. But even here, his syntax should show strain. If a future pass touches ch06, compress his longer sentences into shorter bursts with more restarts.

## Strengths

1. Lena's voice arc is the manuscript's defining achievement. The transition from warm curiosity ("Some people could accept bedrock. Lena never could." ch01:161) through clinical notation ("she should feel cold" family) to raw emotional return ("I can feel it again. I can feel everything." ch14:545) is tracked through prose texture rather than exposition.
2. Webb's "She can't hear it" / "She can hear it now" inversion is structurally precise and emotionally devastating. His three-beat Part III arc provides satisfying resolution.
3. Instructor voices are differentiated after v11.0/v13.1: Yuki is imperative ("Stop. That's your limit."), Thomas is experiential/damaged (flexes his hand, shows his scars), Sarah is scholarly ("This is where the literature diverges").
4. The Yog-Sothoth voice achieves genuine alienness. The three registers (geometric displacement, perceptual synesthesia, subject-object dissolution) are distinct from Nyarlathotep's conversational propositional style.
5. David Chen's voice is appropriately deliberate -- "Truth-seeking feels the same as obsession. Same texture. You have to learn the difference in your hands, not your head." (ch06:119) has the right weight and physical metaphor language.
