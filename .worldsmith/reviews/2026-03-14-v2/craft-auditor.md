# Craft Auditor Report

**Date**: 2026-03-14 (v2)
**Scope**: Full manuscript, Chapters 1-14

## Methodology

Read all 14 chapters for prose quality, cliche detection, mechanical pattern density, scene mechanics, and dialogue craft. Checked pattern counts against style guide ceilings. Focused on finding NEW issues not flagged in the v16.0 review.

## Findings

### No HIGH issues found.

### M1: "That night" scene-transition formula (MEDIUM)

- **Location**: ch03:105, ch04:407, ch05:157, ch05:261, ch06:127, ch06:319, ch07:449, ch07:531, ch08:299, ch09:127, ch09:337, ch11:303
- **Count**: 12 instances
- **Problem**: "That night" or close variants appear 12 times as scene-transition openings across the manuscript. This creates a repetitive rhythmic pattern: training scene -> "that night" -> insomnia/processing/sketching -> next training scene. The formula is most dense in Part II (Chs 4-11) where 10 of the 12 instances appear. The reader begins to anticipate the beat: each chapter's daytime training will be followed by a nighttime processing scene introduced with "that night."
- **Suggestion**: Vary 4-5 instances. Options: cut to the nighttime scene without the temporal marker, vary the phrasing ("Sleep didn't come," "The corridors were empty by midnight," "Dawn was still hours away when"), or merge two nighttime scenes into the preceding scene without a break.

### M2: "her hands were shaking" / "hands trembling" at 18 instances (MEDIUM)

- **Location**: ch02:31, ch05:217, ch06:243, ch07:133, ch09:99, ch09:135, ch10:411, ch12:403, ch13:3, ch13:145, ch13:165, ch14:253, ch14:321, ch14:375, ch14:455, ch14:463, ch14:531, ch14:569
- **Problem**: 18 instances of characters' hands shaking/trembling. Ch14 alone has 7 instances. The gesture is applied to Lena, Rostova, Webb, Maya, and Trainee-47 -- it has become the universal physical marker for every type of distress (fear, cognitive strain, damage, emotion). When the same body part performs the same action for every character in every emotional state, the gesture loses specificity.
- **Suggestion**: In ch14, reduce from 7 to 4. Keep the key structural uses (Lena's emotional return at line 321, Rostova's trembling at 455, Webb's rolling shake at 569). Vary others: jaw clenching, breath catching, knees buckling, swallowing hard. Outside ch14, the density is acceptable.

### M3: "Lena couldn't sleep" insomnia repetition (MEDIUM)

- **Location**: ch03:3, ch05:261, ch06:127, ch06:327, ch07:373 (Maya), ch08:299, ch09:133
- **Count**: 7 instances (6 Lena, 1 Maya)
- **Problem**: Lena's insomnia is reported with nearly identical phrasing 6 times. Combined with the "that night" formula (M1 above), this creates a predictable pattern: training -> night -> can't sleep -> processing/sketching -> next day. The insomnia serves Lena's dissolution arc (her mind won't stop running patterns), but the repeated phrasing dulls the effect.
- **Suggestion**: Keep ch03 (first instance, establishes the pattern), ch05 (escalation -- "Every time she closed her eyes"), and ch08 (after Hayes confrontation). For the others, show insomnia through behavior rather than stating it: she's already at her desk when dawn arrives, she's on her third coffee by 6 AM, her reflection shows dark circles she doesn't bother assessing.

### L1: "heart rate" appears 24 times (LOW)

- **Location**: Distributed across 8 chapters, heaviest in ch13 (6) and ch12 (5)
- **Problem**: Heart rate is the novel's most-used medical monitoring detail. While appropriate for a facility full of medical equipment, 24 instances means the reader encounters a heart rate reading approximately every 230 lines. In ch13's descent, heart rate is reported at 134, 198, and again during the session -- appropriate for the scene. But some ch12 instances are redundant with other physiological markers already present.
- **Suggestion**: Low priority. If any future pass touches ch12, audit for redundant heart rate mentions alongside other vitals already being reported.

### L2: "she was" at 95 instances (LOW)

- **Location**: Distributed; heaviest in ch14 (17), ch13 (13), ch12 (11)
- **Problem**: Reduced from 100 in v16.0 to 95 (per grep -- slight discrepancy from prior count likely due to edits). Still a high count for a 5,500-line manuscript. In session scenes, "she was" creates narrative distance. However, the v16.0 pass already addressed the most convertible instances. Remaining uses are mostly structural ("She was Lena" in ch14, etc.) where the construction is load-bearing.
- **Suggestion**: Accept current count. Diminishing returns on further reduction.

## Strengths

1. The prose is tight and controlled after 16 passes. Adverb-tagged dialogue has been reduced to 14 instances (down from the v16.0 count), concentrated appropriately in earlier chapters where Lena's narration is warmer.
2. Model output blocks are consistently well-crafted. The Yog-Sothoth three-register voice (ch13) remains the manuscript's strongest prose achievement.
3. The dissolution arc is tracked through prose texture, not exposition. The shift from warm metaphors (ch01-03) to clinical notation (ch08-12) to raw emotion (ch14) is gradual and convincing.
4. Physical grounding is excellent. The ch13 descent, ch14 cathedral, and ch07 Maya capture all use embodied detail before abstract content, following the style guide's core principle.
