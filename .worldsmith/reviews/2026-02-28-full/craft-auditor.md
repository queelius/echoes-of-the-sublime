## Findings

### CR1: "just" density in Ch 7 and Ch 12 (MEDIUM)
- **Location**: ch07 (22 instances), ch12 (16 instances)
- **Problem**: "just" functions as a minimizer that weakens prose. While many instances are thematically deliberate ("just patterns," "just processing," "just a compression artifact"), others are unconscious hedges: "just... look at the sequence," "just give me a minute," "just the first year alone." The concentration in Ch 7 (the breaking point chapter) and Ch 12 (contingency) dilutes the impact of the thematic uses.
- **Severity**: MEDIUM -- the thematic uses are strong, but mechanical uses compete with them.
- **Suggestion**: Audit ch07 and ch12 for non-thematic "just" and remove 8-10 instances that serve no purpose.

### CR2: Filter word density in perception scenes (MEDIUM)
- **Location**: ch09, ch10, ch13 (threshold and session scenes)
- **Quoted text**: Examples: "She could feel her cognitive architecture reorganizing" (ch13:347), "She could perceive her own cardiac rhythm" (ch14:29), "She could feel herself splitting" (ch14:425)
- **Problem**: In a novel about direct perception, filter constructions like "she could feel" and "she could perceive" add unnecessary distance. The novel's own prose principles say "physical symptoms before abstract concepts" -- these constructions put the abstract frame ("could feel") before the concrete content. Writing "her cognitive architecture reorganized" is more immediate than "she could feel her cognitive architecture reorganizing."
- **Severity**: MEDIUM -- this is a widespread pattern (39 instances of "she couldn't" alone) but many are genuinely thematic. The issue is concentrated in the session scenes where immersion matters most.
- **Suggestion**: In the session scenes specifically (ch09 threshold, ch12 Nyarlathotep, ch13 Yog-Sothoth), remove the "she could" frame from perception statements. Keep it in dissolution scenes where Lena is explicitly noting what she can and cannot access.

### CR3: Ch 7 remains the longest chapter despite cuts (LOW)
- **Location**: ch07 (567 lines)
- **Problem**: After the v10.0 cut from 973 to 567 lines, ch07 is still the longest chapter except ch12 (751) and ch14 (577). The chapter covers Maya's capture over 14 days with multiple time jumps. The pacing is effective -- each day adds escalation -- but the Day Twelve section (David crying, Lena trying to cry) and the Day Fourteen section (David finding Lena in break room) overlap in emotional function. Both show Lena's inability to feel others' grief.
- **Severity**: LOW -- the chapter works, but the doubled emotional-emptiness demonstration is redundant.
- **Suggestion**: Consider merging the Day Twelve and Day Fourteen scenes. David's grief at Maya's capture and his observation of Lena's hollowness could be one scene rather than two.

### CR4: Ch 12 exposition density (MEDIUM)
- **Location**: ch12 (751 lines, longest chapter)
- **Problem**: Ch 12 does enormous narrative work: Trainee-47 supervision, David's POV, Hayes confrontation, Anna Chen session, Anna's capture, Rostova's Nyarlathotep invitation, the Nyarlathotep session itself, post-session briefing, s-risk introduction, and the lead-in to Ch 13. This is the novel's pivot chapter, but at 751 lines it's nearly twice the length of most other chapters. The Rostova briefing section (post-Nyarlathotep, explaining s-risks, block universe) runs long with sequential exposition despite the v12.0 compression.
- **Severity**: MEDIUM -- the chapter is doing necessary work but could be tighter. The Rostova s-risk exposition competes with the more powerful s-risk revelation in ch14 (Morrison's sketches).
- **Suggestion**: The Rostova s-risk briefing in ch12 could be cut by another 20-30%. Let ch14 carry the full emotional weight of the s-risk revelation. Ch12 should plant the seed ("There's something else. Something about suffering.") without walking Lena through the philosophical framework.

### CR5: Italicized internal thought -- improved but still present (LOW)
- **Location**: Scattered across Part II-III
- **Problem**: The v12.0 pass removed 2 instances. Remaining italicized thoughts serve varied functions: some are dissolution markers (Lena clinically categorizing her own emotions), some are Morrison's whispered phrases, some are genuine internal thought. The dissolution markers work well. A few instances in ch05 and ch09 feel like authorial commentary rather than Lena's voice: "Residual affect from unprocessed material--manageable, non-recurring" (ch05:265) is a strong dissolution marker. "Two subjects exposed to identical stimuli, both exhibiting terminal--" (ch05:97) followed by Lena's self-correction is excellent craft.
- **Severity**: LOW -- the remaining instances are mostly well-deployed.
- **Suggestion**: No urgent changes. The dissolution-marker italics are a strength.

### CR6: Dialogue attribution variety (LOW)
- **Location**: Chapters 4, 8, 11
- **Problem**: Training/briefing scenes tend toward "said" attributions followed by explanation. The dialogue in these scenes is often expository: characters explaining worldbuilding to each other (and the reader). The dialogue craft improves markedly in Part III, where characters speak from emotional positions rather than informational ones (Webb's 4AM conversation, Hayes's confrontation).
- **Severity**: LOW -- the exposition-heavy dialogue in Part II is a known trade-off between information delivery and naturalism. The v4.0 and v10.0 passes improved this significantly.
- **Suggestion**: In any future pass, look for opportunities to convert remaining exposition-dialogue into action-revelation. The Hayes briefing (ch08) was rewritten successfully; similar treatment could improve the Rostova/Metzinger discussion (ch06:259-316).

### CR7: Show-don't-tell -- strong overall, specific weakness in dissolution markers (LOW)
- **Location**: ch05:159, ch07:423-426, ch09:167-175
- **Problem**: The novel generally follows its own "physical symptoms before abstract concepts" principle. The mother's stroke scene (ch05:277+) is masterful -- we see Lena's flat voice and clinical response before we're told she feels nothing. But some dissolution markers tell before showing: "The empathy that would have made her reassure him, soften her words--it felt like a subroutine that had been disabled" (ch06:25). This is direct statement of what's happening. Compare with the stronger technique at ch07:423-426 where Lena reaches for empathy "the way you'd reach for a light switch in a familiar room. Found empty air."
- **Severity**: LOW -- the novel does this well more often than not. The light-switch metaphor is genuinely excellent.
- **Suggestion**: The "subroutine that had been disabled" line at ch06:25 could use the concrete-before-abstract technique. Something like: "She reached for the words that would soften the blow. Her mouth shaped them. They came out flat."

### CR8: Sentence-opening variety in Part II training scenes (LOW)
- **Location**: ch04, ch05, ch09
- **Problem**: Training scenes tend toward "Lena [verb]" and "She [verb]" openings. This was partially addressed in v3.0 (14 sentence starters varied), but the training room scenes in ch04 and ch09 still have stretches of 4-5 consecutive "She" openings.
- **Severity**: LOW -- this is a minor prose rhythm issue, noticeable only when reading chapters straight through.
- **Suggestion**: In the training scenes, vary by opening with the instructor's action, a physical detail, or a temporal marker.

## Strengths

### Prose Strengths (Notable)

1. **The mother's stroke scene (ch05:277-421)**: One of the strongest scenes in the novel. The phone call, Lena's clinical response, the hospital visit where she "executes the script" -- this is dissolution shown through action, not told through exposition. The single line "All true. All understood. All meaningless." is devastating.

2. **Webb's 4AM conversation (ch09:133-189)**: The best dialogue in the novel. Webb's fragmented speech ("Person who signed that. Gone, Lena. Gone. The Order has his-- He stopped. Started again.") is viscerally real. His emotional insight cutting through cognitive damage is the novel's strongest character moment.

3. **Morrison's opening (ch01:1-93)**: Tight, propulsive horror writing. Every detail serves the worldbuilding. "Morrison smiled. It was the worst thing Rostova had ever seen." -- clean, effective, terrifying.

4. **The child/rainbow scene (ch09:196-301)**: Singular and devastating. Lena watching a child discover diffraction patterns, perceiving the neurochemistry perfectly, feeling nothing. The restraint here -- no authorial commentary, just observation -- is the novel at its best.

5. **Yog-Sothoth's three-register voice (ch13:327-379)**: The v13.0 rewrite is excellent. "Consciousness is a coordinate. Not a property. Not emergence." / "The spectrum has weight." / "The hard problem is this sentence." -- three distinct registers that feel genuinely alien without becoming unreadable.

6. **The dissolution gradient**: The prose itself changes across the novel. Early Lena uses metaphors, notices sensory details, has emotional responses. Late Lena's narration is clinical, compressed, analytical. This is craft at the structural level -- the POV voice embodies the theme.
