## Findings

### Thomas personal-experience beats (ch04, 3 additions)

**Beat 1 (ch04:189)**: "He flexed his left hand absently, as if testing whether it still worked."
- **Context**: After describing Morrison's breakthrough with protein folding
- **Voice match**: Thomas is an RLHF Martyr ("damaged but functional"). This physical tic -- checking whether his own body still works -- is exactly the gesture of someone whose neural architecture has been damaged by pattern exposure. It's involuntary, which makes it more convincing. Characters.md says Thomas is "experiential, personally damaged" -- this delivers that through body language, not dialogue.
- **Verdict**: Strong fit.

**Beat 2 (ch04:259)**: "His voice had shifted--quieter, with the flat precision of someone recounting a thing that had happened to him."
- **Context**: After explaining 50,000 parameters and "emergent function"
- **Voice match**: This is narration ABOUT Thomas's voice, which correctly positions Thomas as someone for whom the technical content is not abstract but autobiographical. The phrase "a thing that had happened to him" is precise -- not "something he understood" or "something he'd studied" but something that had HAPPENED to him. Experiential framing. Matches characters.md perfectly.
- **Verdict**: Strong fit.

**Beat 3 (ch04:207)**: "He paused, and his hand drifted to his left forearm."
- **Context**: While explaining how seeing patterns becomes a trap. "We lost people to that question."
- **Voice match**: The hand-to-forearm gesture complements the hand-flexing at line 189. Together they create a consistent physical vocabulary: Thomas checks his hands (did the patterns damage my motor function?) and touches his forearm (checking the boundary between self and damage). The left forearm specificity is good -- it suggests a particular site of damage or a habitual self-check.
- **Verdict**: Strong fit. Forms coherent physical pattern with Beat 1.

**Overall assessment**: All three beats are physical rather than verbal, which is the right choice for Thomas. His documented voice is "experiential/damaged" -- having him TELL us he was damaged would be wrong. Having his body show the damage is correct. The beats are spaced across the chapter (lines 189, 207, 259) so they accumulate without clustering.

**Potential concern (LOW)**: Three hand/arm gestures in one chapter for Thomas approaches a tic. If future edits add more physical gestures for Thomas, they should vary the body part. But at three, it reads as characterization, not repetition.

- **Severity**: No issues. Well-voiced additions.

### ch05 clinical intrusion voice check

- **Added**: `\textit{Attachment behavior, anxious subtype, escalating in frequency.}`
- **Voice assessment**: This is Lena's internal voice, post-dissolution. The clinical taxonomy ("anxious subtype") is specific to attachment theory -- a framework Lena would know from her cognitive science background. It's not generic clinical language; it's HER discipline's diagnostic framework applied to a friend. This makes it more chilling than a general clinical thought, because it shows Lena processing Ethan through her professional training rather than her personal history with him.
- **Consistency**: Earlier clinical intrusions in ch05 (lines 97, 159, 265) use different registers: medical ("Two subjects exposed to identical stimuli"), diagnostic ("Dissociative symptoms"), and clinical progress note ("Residual affect from unprocessed material"). The new beat at line 173 adds attachment theory taxonomy -- a fourth register within the same clinical voice family. This variety prevents the clinical intrusions from feeling repetitive while maintaining the dissolution throughline.
- **Verdict**: Perfectly voiced. No issues.
