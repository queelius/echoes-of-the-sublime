### What Needs Review
- **Wuzhun's name**: "Wuzhun" is the name of a historical Chan master (Wuzhun Shifan, 1178-1249) who had connections to Japan. The story's Wuzhun is fictional but the name has real associations. Consider whether this is a problem.
- **Tofukuji reference**: Tofukuji was a real Gozan temple in Kyoto, established 1236. Appropriate for 1340s setting.
- **Translation chain**: The three-language relay (Tibetan > Chinese > Japanese) is historically plausible for this period but may strain credulity for the specificity of communication depicted. The story handles this by noting that "language was not the medium that mattered."
- **Kaido's method**: The yamabushi cold-water practice (takigyo) is real Shugendo practice. Using it as an attempted intervention for Ensho is fictional but consistent with the tradition's approach.
- **The monk from Echizen**: The account of the nun who sat for forty days references a real pattern in Zen hagiography (extended sitting as spiritual achievement). The ambiguity about whether her death was failure or success is deliberate.
- **The retrieved monk**: The account of a monk pulled back who "never sat zazen again" raises the question of whether intervention is merciful or destructive. This is left deliberately unresolved.
