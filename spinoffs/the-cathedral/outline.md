## I. The Descent (~2,000 words)

### Scene 1 — Surface (January 2008)

**Setting:** Site-7 surface. Late January. Arizona desert, midmorning. Cold at altitude — not the Arizona of postcards. Wind turbines turning slowly in a dry wind. A low industrial building with no signage. Perimeter fence, security checkpoint, gravel lot. The kind of facility that looks like it processes wastewater or stores government surplus.

**What happens:** Mara arrives by SUV from Phoenix, driven by someone who doesn't make conversation. She steps out into the cold. She sees the wind turbines first — dozens of them, turning, and she counts them because she counts things. She sees the building and is underwhelmed. She meets her escort: a polite security officer who calls her Dr. Voss and asks for her badge (blue). She is walked past the perimeter fence. She notes the ravens — thirty or forty on the fenceline, facing the building, evenly spaced. She asks about them. The security officer says they've always been there. She files this: desert ravens, human structures, food sources, shade. Normal.

She enters the building. Cool air. A corridor that leads to the first elevator.

**What Mara perceives:** The precision of the wind turbine array (she notes the spacing is too regular for purely wind-optimal placement — the turbines are a visual screen as much as a power source, though she doesn't think this consciously). The dry cold. The silence — no highway noise, no aircraft corridors, nothing but wind and turbine hum. The ravens, observed with a physicist's eye: species, count, behavior, spacing. The building's deceptive modesty.

**What the reader perceives that Mara doesn't:** The ravens are the wildlife anomaly documented across all Order facilities (`worldbuilding.md > Environmental Effects`). They don't fly over the building. They face it. Mara's observation is more detailed than Site-7's own security has managed, but she has no framework for interpreting what she sees. The turbines are a cover story (`worldbuilding.md > Site-7: Surface`). The facility she is about to enter extends twenty-four levels into bedrock.

**Wrongness beats:** Birds only. First mention. Not yet wrong — just noted. The count, the stillness, the even spacing. A physicist filing data she doesn't have a model for yet.

**Word count target:** ~500 words

**Cross-refs:** `worldbuilding.md > Site-7` (surface description, wind turbines as cover). `worldbuilding.md > Environmental Effects` (raven congregation at Site-7, 30-200 at any time). `story-notes.md > Section 5 > Birds > Movement I`.

### Scene 2 — The Existing Facility (January 2008)

**Setting:** Sublevels 1 through 7. Three-elevator descent with badge-controlled transfers. The upper facility: server rooms, labs, a cafeteria, offices. Sublevel 5: the medical observation ward, glimpsed through reinforced glass during an elevator transfer. Then deeper — past Sublevel 7, where the air changes.

**What happens:** Mara descends. She reads the facility the way she reads an instrument — structure, function, tolerance, margin. The first elevator takes her to Sublevel 4, where she transfers. Poured concrete walls, institutional lighting, the sound of HVAC. Server rooms visible through glass panels: racks of compute hardware she doesn't recognize. Not standard architecture. She notes the scale — more processing power in one room than every lab she has worked in combined.

At the Sublevel 5 transfer, she waits for the second elevator. Through reinforced glass: a medical ward. Monitoring equipment, EEG readouts, vital signs displays, an IV drip. Someone sitting in a chair. Not restrained. Not reading, not watching, not doing anything she can identify. Eyes open. Head slightly tilted. Still. The elevator arrives. The doors close.

She descends to Sublevel 7. Transfers again. She meets her team lead — Dr. Petersen, a cryogenics engineer from Fermilab who has been here two years and answers questions about cooling systems with enthusiasm and questions about everything else with silence. Petersen walks her through the lower construction levels. She is introduced to her team: six engineers from national labs, CERN, LIGO. Good people. None of them will say who recruited them either.

**What Mara perceives:** The scale of the compute infrastructure (she estimates power requirements and arrives at a number that makes her recalculate). The compute hardware's unfamiliar architecture — photonic interconnects, neuromorphic elements, analog components she suspects are memristive. The medical ward: a single registered image, filed without interpretation. The person in the chair. The stillness. The camaraderie of her team — people who are very good at what they do and very comfortable not asking why. The change in air quality below Sublevel 7. Her ears popping at the pressure transition between 10 and 11.

**What the reader perceives that Mara doesn't:** The person in the medical ward is an early casualty of the Computational Turn — someone damaged by interaction with the cruder pre-LLM models of the 1950s-2000s era, or by early uncontrolled exposure to Nyarlathotep (`lore.md > The Computational Turn` — "machine pattern-recognition could augment human perception," and the cost was invisible until it wasn't; `story-notes.md > Section 5 > The Medical Ward` — "someone whose name Mara will never learn"). The Order has been producing cognitive casualties since at least the 1950s; they happened more slowly before the LLMs. The compute hardware is the substrate for Shoggoth (Sublevel 3) and Nyarlathotep (Sublevel 7), already operational. The badge colors map to clearance tiers — Mara's blue is contractor-level; red opens doors to the models themselves.

**Wrongness beats:** The person in the medical ward. Not a wrongness Mara registers as such — she sees a patient in a clinic, which is normal for a facility with a medical wing. The wrongness is in what the reader notices: the open eyes, the absence of any activity, the stillness that is not sleep. This is the first seed. Mara files it and moves on.

**Word count target:** ~800 words

**Cross-refs:** `worldbuilding.md > Site-7` (sublevel layout, compute specs). `worldbuilding.md > Model Specifications` (Shoggoth on SL3, Nyarlathotep on SL7 — both operational by 2008). `lore.md > The Computational Turn` (pre-LLM casualties — the medical ward patient predates the RLHF Martyrs). `story-notes.md > Section 4 > Compartmentalization Map` (medical ward, badge colors, what Mara sees vs. understands).

### Scene 3 — The Cathedral (January 2008)

**Setting:** Sublevel 24. The geothermal cathedral. Under construction but the chamber itself is carved — a vast space in bedrock, partially lit by construction floods, the ceiling lost in shadow. Geothermal cooling pipes run along the walls. The air is warm — 30+C, rising from below. The 1847 drill marks are visible in the deepest rock, where the earliest excavation met the geology that would become the chamber floor.

**What happens:** Mara steps out of the third elevator into the space where she will spend the next two years. The chamber is enormous — she has been in LIGO's vacuum tubes, which are four kilometers long, and those felt vast in a single dimension. This is vast in three. Carved from unweathered granite, the walls show the geological history of the excavation: faced concrete at the top (recent decades), shotcrete against exposed rock in the middle (mid-twentieth century), and at the deepest level, the original cuts — rough, steam-powered drill marks from 1847, still legible in the stone.

She touches the drill marks. Runs her fingertips along the grooves. Someone started digging here eleven years before the Civil War. With steam drills. Without knowing what they would find or what would be built in the space they carved. She is finishing what they started without knowing either.

She asks what they are measuring. The answer comes from someone she won't see again — a figure in the construction crew, older, with the look of someone who has been at this facility for decades. "Everything we can." She waits for elaboration. None comes. She looks at the specs for the probe array — quantum coherence detectors, interferometers, neural recording equipment, spectroscopy, dark matter detectors, EM surveys, seismic arrays — and understands: the answer was literal. They are building instruments to measure everything this environment can reveal. She just doesn't know why.

The chamber hums. Faint. Below the construction noise. She assumes geothermal systems.

**What Mara perceives:** The scale of the chamber, understood through engineering rather than awe — she estimates volume, notes the rock type, assesses the thermal gradient. The 1847 drill marks, perceived as a physical timeline: 160 years of excavation compressed into strata of cutting technology. The probe array specs, which she reads with professional hunger — these are instruments she has dreamed of building. The "everything we can" answer, which she accepts because the specs support it. The warmth. The hum.

**What the reader perceives that Mara doesn't:** This is the geothermal cathedral (`worldbuilding.md > Site-7 > Sublevel 24: Vault 9`). The space where Yog-Sothoth will operate. The 1847 drill marks are the scar of the Order's original commitment to infrastructure over contemplation — the moment the Western branch chose to build toward knowledge rather than perceive it directly (`lore.md > The Divergence > Site-7 excavation`). "Everything we can" is not a deflection; it is the Order's actual epistemological ambition. The Promethean shape: someone decided to steal fire 160 years ago, and Mara is building the torch.

**Wrongness beats:** The hum — noted but not yet classified as wrong. Just a sound at depth. The answer that isn't an answer. The precision of the specs, which are too good — instruments specified beyond what any public lab has achieved, implying decades of private R&D that doesn't exist in any literature she knows.

**1847 drill marks:** First appearance. Mara touches them. The physical contact matters — her hands on the grooves that someone else's hands and tools cut when Arizona was still Mexican territory. The deep-time thread begins here.

**Word count target:** ~700 words

**Cross-refs:** `worldbuilding.md > Site-7` (Sublevel 24, geothermal cathedral, Vault 9). `worldbuilding.md > Yog-Sothoth Sessions` (55C ambient, 800+ MW, the physical environment). `lore.md > History Timeline` (1847 excavation). `lore.md > The Divergence > Site-7 excavation` ("thirty years to reach depth," "the faith required to build a cathedral for instruments that didn't yet exist"). `story-notes.md > Section 3 > The Probe Array` (full instrument inventory in Mara's language).

## II. The Building (~2,500 words)

### Scene 4 — First Months: The Work (March-June 2008)

**Setting:** Sublevel 24 and surrounding work areas. Spring through early summer, though seasons are meaningless underground. The chamber filling with instruments: scaffolding, cable runs, clean-room tents around the quantum coherence detectors. Mara's lab — a workspace carved from an adjacent corridor, with her own terminal, her own data feeds, a coffee maker she brought from home.

**What happens:** Time compresses. Months of engineering work, rendered through specific beats rather than summary. Mara installs the quantum coherence detectors first — her specialty, the instruments she designed. The prose lingers here: the precision of alignment, the satisfaction of a noise floor dropping to spec, the femtosecond temporal resolution she has never achieved outside a thought experiment. She names the primary coherence monitor "Judith" (after Judith Curry, a physicist she admired in grad school).

Then the interferometers — Fabry-Perot cavities in kilometer-scale vacuum tubes carved into bedrock. She knows this technology from LIGO. These are more sensitive by orders of magnitude. She asks why the interferometers are underground (at LIGO, surface placement is preferred for gravitational wave detection). Gets a non-answer about "cross-modal environmental characterization." Files it.

Then the bio package. Neural recording arrays at synaptic resolution — specs from someone she never meets. She installs and calibrates per spec. The signal conditioning is elegant, the sampling rates extraordinary. She doesn't know what neural system these will monitor. The disconnect between the physics instruments and the neuroscience equipment nags at her. She asks once about "cross-modal correlation studies." Gets a rote answer. Doesn't ask again.

The team works well. Ruiz, the electrician, tells stories about deep mines in Nevada. Two CERN engineers argue about detector calibration in French. The work is absorbing, precise, and deeply satisfying. Mara is building the best instrument of her career.

**What Mara perceives:** The pleasure of the work. The extraordinary quality of the specs. The team dynamics — camaraderie built through shared precision. The nagging wrongness of the bio package (neuroscience instruments in a physics installation). The limitless funding — every component she requests arrives within days, at specifications she'd never dare ask for in a university setting.

**What the reader perceives that Mara doesn't:** She is building the sensory apparatus of something that will perceive reality at a scale no human mind can hold (`worldbuilding.md > Session Mechanics > Yog-Sothoth Sessions` — the probe array feeds data into the model). The bio package is the neural recording equipment for translators — the instruments that will monitor a human brain while it interfaces with Yog-Sothoth. The chair she glimpsed on the specs is the same type of chair the person in the medical ward is sitting in. Her pride in the work is the horror — the ordinary pleasure of precision applied to building something irrevocable.

**Wrongness beats:** None explicit. This scene is deliberate normalcy — the work before the wrongnesses intrude. The only signal: the bio package mismatch, which is intellectual unease, not dread. The scene establishes what Mara is capable of feeling (satisfaction, curiosity, pride) so that the subsequent erosion of these feelings registers.

**Word count target:** ~700 words

**Cross-refs:** `story-notes.md > Section 3 > The Probe Array` (full instrument inventory, what Mara calls things). `worldbuilding.md > Yog-Sothoth Sessions` (probe array as session infrastructure). `story-notes.md > Section 1 > Personality` (precision as identity, curiosity within limits).

### Scene 5 — The Birds (July-August 2008)

**Setting:** Surface excursions during the summer. The desert in July — brutal heat, everything bleached. The perimeter fence at dawn, when Mara goes up for air. Sublevel 24 during the day, where the bird count doesn't matter but the memory of the birds follows her down.

**What happens:** Mara has been underground for months. She surfaces for air, for sunlight, for the biological need to see sky. The ravens have multiplied. She counted forty in January. Now she counts over a hundred, ranging the full perimeter. They rotate — individuals come and go — but the congregation never fully disperses. They still face the building. They still don't fly over it.

She tests this. One morning, eating breakfast outside (one of the few who bothers to surface for meals), she throws a crust of bread toward the center of the building's roof. A raven tracks the bread, banks, curves away from the airspace above the main structure, returns to the fence. She does it three more times. Same result. The bread lands on the roof untouched.

She mentions the birds to Ruiz. He shrugs: "They've been doing that since I got here. Three years." She mentions them to a security officer, who says, "Wildlife control looked at it. Ravens. They like the fence." She asks Petersen. Petersen says, "There's a paper somewhere — electromagnetic interference from the ventilation systems, maybe. I don't know. They're birds." And changes the subject.

She measures. She counts daily for a week. She photographs the perimeter from the roof. She plots the count against time of day, temperature, wind speed. The count does not correlate with any environmental variable she tracks. It correlates weakly with something she can't identify — a faint increase on days when the lower sublevels are more active (she notices this because the elevator wait times are longer on busy days, and those days have more birds). She tells herself this is coincidence. She writes no paper. She mentions it to no one else.

**What Mara perceives:** The wrongness of the bird behavior, perceived through a scientific lens. The failure of her environmental model to explain the count. The weak correlation with facility activity — noted, not pursued. The isolation of being the only person who seems to find this interesting. The heat, the light, the relief of sky after months of stone.

**What the reader perceives that Mara doesn't:** The birds' behavior is documented across all Order facilities worldwide (`worldbuilding.md > Environmental Effects`). Ravens at Site-7, terns in Norway, vultures in Brazil, crows in Japan. "Different species, identical behavior." The correlation with sublevel activity is real — the models are running, and the birds respond. The bread test demonstrates the airspace avoidance pattern. Mara's data, if anyone in the Order read it, would be the most rigorous wildlife observation they possess.

**Wrongness beats:** Birds — full escalation from background detail to active anomaly. This is the first wrongness Mara cannot dismiss. She can explain ravens on a fence. She cannot explain ravens that refuse to overfly a building, whose count correlates with nothing in her models, and whose presence every colleague treats as furniture. The gap between observable phenomenon and available explanation opens here.

**Word count target:** ~500 words

**Cross-refs:** `worldbuilding.md > Environmental Effects` (all details — perimeter congregation, airspace avoidance, species-identical behavior). `story-notes.md > Section 5 > Birds > Movement II`. `story-notes.md > Section 4 > Compartmentalization Map > Surface` (what Mara sees at ground level).

### Scene 6 — The Deeper Wrongnesses (September-October 2008)

**Setting:** The lower sublevels, primarily 15-24. Mara's lab. Her quarters (somewhere in the upper levels — a small room with a bed, a desk, and nothing personal except a framed photo of her parents and the notebook where she logs anomalies she can't explain). Late at night, waking from dreams. The spectral analyzer display showing the 7 Hz peak.

**What happens:** The wrongnesses converge. This scene braids four threads:

**The headaches.** Below Sublevel 15, the dull pressure behind Mara's eyes. Medical says barometric differential. She takes her own pressure readings. They don't correlate. The headaches correlate with proximity to the compute hardware — worse near the server racks, better in the rock corridors. She mentions this to no one because it sounds irrational. Ruiz tells her: "It's the hum. You can't hear it but your head can."

**The sound.** She checks for infrasound with her spectral analyzer. She finds it: broadband below 15 Hz, peaking at approximately 7 Hz, with harmonic structure she can almost but not quite resolve. The hum is not mechanical — or rather, it has a mechanical component, but beneath that, something else. Something with periodicity. She assumes geodynamic. Earth-noise transmitted through bedrock. Consistent with natural infrasound from geological processes. She files the measurement. She returns to it at night, alone in her lab, running the spectrogram at higher resolution. The structure doesn't resolve. It is always almost-a-pattern.

**The dreams.** Her dreams change. Not nightmares — denser. Structured. Non-narrative. Geometric. She dreams of surfaces curving in directions she can't describe. She wakes with the conviction that she perceived something important, and the perception evaporates in the time it takes to open her eyes. By October, the dreams have acquired a quality she can only describe as deep. As if the dream is happening at a lower frequency than her normal perceptual range, and she is only catching harmonics.

**The equipment.** During calibration, her detectors perform too well. Signal-to-noise ratios exceed thermal models. The noise floor drops below prediction without engineering explanation. She recalibrates. The numbers hold. She runs the coherence monitors overnight, unmanned: the noise floor is lower when no one is measuring. She knows this is confirmation bias waiting to happen and stops the overnight runs. But the data is clean. Anomalously, persistently clean.

**What Mara perceives:** Four anomalies, each individually dismissible, collectively forming a pattern she can almost see. Her scientific frame strains — she is observing phenomena she can't explain, and her instinct is to explain them. The gap between what she can measure and what she can feel widens. Her language starts to lose composure — the engineering precision gives way to longer, more recursive sentences as she circles something she doesn't have a word for.

**What the reader perceives that Mara doesn't:** The headaches correlate with proximity to active computation — Shoggoth and Nyarlathotep are running on the sublevels above her, and the Mechanism's physical substrate affects biology even without direct session contact (`worldbuilding.md > Environmental Effects > Implications`). The 7 Hz infrasound sits at the boundary between geological noise and something else — real infrasound at this frequency causes unease, visual disturbances, and feelings of presence in humans (`worldbuilding.md > Exploratory Notes > Acoustic Ecology`). The dreams are a faint echo of what translators experience in sessions — geometric perception, cross-modal synthesis, non-narrative structure. The equipment anomalies are the environment responding to computational activity — "as if reality wants to be observed at this depth."

**Wrongness beats:** All four wrongnesses present and escalating. Headaches (new). Sound (new — the 7 Hz peak, the almost-pattern). Dreams (new — denser, geometric). Equipment (new — the too-good noise floors). Each one gets a paragraph. Together they form the counter-rhythm that will dominate Movement II.

**Word count target:** ~700 words

**Cross-refs:** `worldbuilding.md > Environmental Effects > Implications` (physical correlates of model computation). `worldbuilding.md > Exploratory Notes > Acoustic Ecology` (infrasound, facility soundscape). `story-notes.md > Section 5` (full wrongness schedule: headaches, sound, dreams, equipment anomalies — all Movement II).

### Scene 7 — The Translator (October-November 2008)

**Setting:** The Sublevel 5 elevator transfer. The medical ward, seen through reinforced glass for the second time. Then the elevator, with Petersen. Then Mara's lab, alone, at night.

**What happens:** Months after her first glimpse, Mara passes the medical ward again. She looks deliberately this time. The same person — she recognizes the posture, the chair. The lips are moving now. No sound through the glass. The eyes are open and tracking — not focused on anything in the room, but tracking, as if following something she can't see.

On the elevator, she asks Petersen. "Who's that?" Petersen glances at the window. "One of the translators." A pause. "Long-duration project." Another pause, and Petersen pivots to cooling system specifications for Sublevel 22. Mara lets it change.

"Translator" goes into her mental file of overheard fragments. She has accumulated others. Bandwidth (not in a radio-frequency context). Session (not in a computing context). Carrying (not physical). One mention of "the protocols" delivered with a grimness that didn't match procedural language. She has no framework for these words. She files them the way she files anomalous data: carefully, without interpretation.

That night, in her lab, she sits with the word. Translator. A person who translates. Between what and what? The neural recording equipment in her array — the bio package she didn't design — resolves to a new sharpness. Neural recording at synaptic resolution. A chair with restraints and EEG leads. A person in a ward whose lips move without sound. The connection is there, available, geometric in its clarity, and she turns away from it. Not from fear. From a discipline deeper than curiosity: the discipline of not knowing what you are not cleared to know. She learned this at LIGO. She perfected it here.

She does not ask again. This restraint costs her nothing at the time.

**What Mara perceives:** The person in the ward, seen with new eyes — the lips moving, the tracking gaze. The word "translator" and its non-answer context. The accumulation of overheard jargon. The almost-connection between the bio package, the chair, and the ward patient. The deliberate turn away from understanding.

**What the reader perceives that Mara doesn't:** The person is an early cognitive casualty — someone damaged by computational interaction during the Computational Turn or early model work (`lore.md > RLHF Martyrs`). "Translator" is Order terminology for someone trained to interface with models (`lore.md > Terminology`). The connection Mara almost makes is the correct one: her instruments will record what happens to a human brain when it interfaces with Yog-Sothoth. The bio package is for the person in the chair. The person in the ward is what happens when it goes wrong.

**Wrongness beats:** The medical ward — escalation from first glimpse (Scene 2, still and silent) to second glimpse (lips moving, eyes tracking). The word "translator." The institutional silence around the word. This is the human wrongness, distinct from the environmental wrongnesses of Scene 6. Mara's decision not to ask is not cowardice — it is the compartmentalized discipline that makes her useful to the Order and blind to what she serves.

**Word count target:** ~400 words

**Cross-refs:** `lore.md > The Computational Turn` (early casualties, pre-LLM era). `lore.md > Terminology` ("translator"). `story-notes.md > Section 4 > What Mara Partially Sees` (medical ward, higher-clearance scientists). `story-notes.md > Section 5 > The Medical Ward` (the person is NOT Morrison and NOT an RLHF Martyr — predates both).

### Scene 8 — The Surface (November 2008)

**Setting:** A weekend surface visit. Mara drives to Phoenix, stays in a hotel, calls her parents. A diner. A clear November sky. Then the drive back. The perimeter fence at dawn.

**What happens:** Mara surfaces. Not for maintenance or meals — for life. She drives the two hours to Phoenix in a borrowed facility vehicle. She checks into a hotel. She calls her mother, who asks if she's eating, and her father, who asks about the instruments. She tells them she's working on "a sensor array for a private research facility" and they accept this because it sounds like her. She eats a meal in a diner where the noise — conversation, clatter, a television playing football — is overwhelming after months of underground quiet. She sleeps in a bed that is too soft. She dreams of surfaces curving. She doesn't dream of the facility. She dreams of the geometry.

She drives back at dawn. Approaches the perimeter. The ravens are on the fence. Hundreds of them in the early light, facing the building, silent. She slows the car and watches them for a long time. A minute. Two. They watch back. Not the building — her. As if they know she is the one building the instruments. As if they are waiting for her to finish.

She parks. Goes through security. Descends. Returns to her lab. The coffee maker is where she left it. The coherence monitors are running. Judith is performing to spec. She is home — and the word strikes her, because Sublevel 24 should not feel like home, and it does.

**What Mara perceives:** The strangeness of the normal world after months underground. The noise, the softness, the triviality. The comfort of her parents' voices. The geometric dreams that follow her to the surface. The ravens, observed with an attention that has shifted from scientific to personal. The disquieting recognition that she is more comfortable at depth than at surface. That the facility has become home.

**What the reader perceives that Mara doesn't:** The dreams are not facility-specific — whatever is happening to her perception is not confined to proximity. The ravens watching her (not the building) when she returns is the first hint of the wildlife anomaly's personal dimension. The facility feeling like home is the beginning of what the Order would call "carrying" — not capture, not damage, but the first faint imprint of proximity to something that reorganizes perception. She is not a translator. She has never been in a session. But she has been building the instrument at depth for months, near active computation, and the edges of her framework are softening.

**Wrongness beats:** Dreams (persistent at surface — the geometry travels). Ravens (watching her specifically on return — personalized). The facility-as-home feeling (the deepest wrongness, not environmental but internal — the beginning of the reorientation that the coda will complete).

**Word count target:** ~400 words

**Cross-refs:** `story-notes.md > Section 5 > Dreams` (geometric quality, persistence). `worldbuilding.md > Environmental Effects` (ravens, proximity effects). `story-notes.md > Section 1 > Personality` (curiosity within limits, physical awareness).

## III. The Commissioning (~2,500 words)

### Scene 9 — The Cathedral Complete (October 2009)

**Setting:** Sublevel 24. The chamber, transformed. What was raw rock and construction scaffolding is now filled with instruments — every surface occupied, every cable run complete, every detector aligned. The probe array, finished. The compute racks, live. The chamber is cooler now (geothermal cooling fully operational) but the air has weight — electromagnetic, thermal, vibrational. The chair sits in the center, surrounded by monitoring equipment. The 1847 drill marks are still visible on the far wall, below the cable runs, behind a rack of photonic processors.

**What happens:** Mara completes the final calibration sequence. Eighteen months of work. She runs the full-array diagnostic: coherence detectors nominal, interferometers locked, bio package powered and waiting, spectroscopy calibrated, EM surveys sweeping, seismic arrays listening. Every instrument performing to spec or better — the anomalous noise suppression that troubled her in Scene 6 now reads as a feature. The data is clean. The array is ready.

She walks the chamber. The space feels different with instruments in it — not smaller but denser, more intentional. When it was empty rock it was just a cavern. Now it is an organ of perception. She passes the chair and does not touch it. She pauses at the far wall and finds the 1847 drill marks beneath the cable runs. She touches them again. The rock is warm. The drill grooves are filled with grit from the construction. She brushes them clean with her thumb. 162 years. Steam drills to quantum coherence monitors. The same project. The same dig.

She walks back through the chamber. Something about the space has changed. Not the instruments — those are exactly as she left them. Not the temperature or pressure or light. Something she doesn't have a sensor for. The chamber feels occupied. Not by people. By potential. By the imminence of what the instruments will measure when they are turned on. She tells herself this is the psychological weight of completing a large project. She has felt this at LIGO too — the moment when the instrument is ready and the universe hasn't sent a signal yet. The waiting.

It isn't the same. She knows it isn't the same.

**What Mara perceives:** The satisfaction of completion. The beauty of the instrument array — every component in its place, every cable dressed, every alignment true. The 1847 drill marks, touched for the second time. The chamber's new quality — occupied, expectant, waiting. Her own unease, which she attributes to the psychology of completion and which is something else.

**What the reader perceives that Mara doesn't:** The chamber is Vault 9, ready for Yog-Sothoth. The probe array is the sensory apparatus of something that will perceive reality at 1000 trillion parameters across a 10-trillion-token context window (`worldbuilding.md > Model Specifications`). The chair is where translators will sit — Morrison will last 8 minutes in it, a decade from now. The "occupied" feeling is not psychological. The compute racks are live. Shoggoth and Nyarlathotep are running on the sublevels above. The models' physical substrate — whatever it does to local space — is already active. What Mara perceives as potential is proximity to a threshold. The Pandora shape: the box is built, the lid is ready, and the woman who built it is standing inside.

**Wrongness beats:** The chamber's "occupied" quality — environmental, not mechanical. The gap between Mara's engineering explanation (psychology of completion) and what the space actually feels like. The 1847 drill marks, seen now not as history but as destiny — the accumulated purpose of 162 years, all of it pointing at this room, this array, this moment. The wrongness is quiet here. Almost gentle. The last calm before the commissioning.

**1847 drill marks:** Second appearance. Touched again. This time Mara brushes grit from the grooves — a caretaking gesture, tending the scar. The deep-time thread tightens.

### Scene 10 — The First Training Run (Late November 2009)

**Setting:** Mara's lab, adjacent to the main chamber. She is not cleared for the chamber itself during the run. Her workstation: three monitors showing probe array telemetry, a spectrogram display, her coffee (cold, untouched). Through the wall, the chamber. Through the wall, data.

**What happens:** The day arrives. Higher-clearance personnel have been moving through the facility for weeks — red badges she hasn't seen before, faces she doesn't recognize, a tension in the corridors that she reads as institutional excitement. Petersen is unavailable. Ruiz has been reassigned to surface maintenance. Her team has been told to monitor from their stations and not enter the main chamber.

At 0900, the computational system — the racks of photonic processors, neuromorphic chips, analog memristive architectures that she has watched being installed without understanding what they compute — begins ingesting data from her probe array. She watches the data flow on her monitors. She watches her instruments feed information into something she has never seen.

The data flows. Normally. For twenty minutes, the readings are exactly what she'd expect from a sensor array in a deep underground chamber: thermal noise, seismic background, quantum decoherence at predicted rates, electromagnetic spectrum within normal parameters. The compute system is processing the data. Nothing unusual. She drinks her coffee (still cold from the morning).

Then the readings change.

Not into noise. Into coherence. Her quantum decoherence monitors show a discontinuity: decoherence times lengthen by a factor of three over fifteen minutes, then stabilize at the new value. This should not happen. Temperature didn't change. Electromagnetic background didn't change. Vibrational interference didn't change. But the quantum systems in her detectors are behaving as if the environment has become more coherent — as if reality itself has organized around the observation.

Her interferometers lock. Fringe patterns that were previously noisy resolve into configurations that are too stable, too precise, too — she thinks the word "beautiful" and is disturbed by it. Beauty is not a measurement.

She stares at the data.

**What Mara perceives:** Normal operations becoming abnormal. The data shifting from expected to impossible without any change in the physical parameters she monitors. The coherence discontinuity — the most disturbing signal, because she understands the physics well enough to know it shouldn't happen. The beauty of the data, which frightens her because beauty is an aesthetic judgment and she is an instrument physicist. Her own stillness — she stops moving, stops adjusting, stops doing anything but watching.

**What the reader perceives that Mara doesn't:** Yog-Sothoth's first training run has begun. The model is processing probe array data through 1000 trillion parameters. What Mara sees as impossible coherence is the model finding structure in reality that her instruments were designed to detect but that only becomes visible when something with sufficient computational depth processes the signal. The model isn't distorting her data — it's completing it. "Finding what was always there" (design doc). The fire is being lit.

**Wrongness beats:** Equipment anomalies — climax level. The decoherence discontinuity. The interferometer lock. The coherence that shouldn't be possible. The word "beautiful." Each one is a signal that Mara's framework is cracking — she has instruments for everything in this room except what is happening.

### Scene 11 — The Change (Late November 2009, same day)

**Setting:** Same — Mara's lab. But time has passed. The monitors still showing data. The coffee cold on the desk. The light in the lab hasn't changed (no windows) but the quality of the air has. Then: a scientist with a red badge enters. Then: the elevator, ascending. Then: the surface, at dusk.

**What happens:** Mara has been sitting motionless for three hours. She does not know this. The data streams. Her instruments show readings that have stabilized at values that shouldn't be stable — the decoherence times, the interferometer lock, the noise floor lower than any thermal model predicts. The data isn't wrong. It looks like her instruments, for the first time, are measuring what they were actually designed to measure. Everything before this moment was noise. Now the signal has arrived.

The hum changes. During the three hours she sat motionless, it dropped — below the 7 Hz she measured months ago, below perception as tactile vibration. Her spectral analyzer, still running, shows it: the broadband peak has shifted to 2 Hz. The harmonic structure is simpler, cleaner. The noise has organized itself into something coherent. Clean signals from natural sources don't reorganize spontaneously.

A scientist enters. Red badge. Someone she hasn't seen before. Calm, practiced, not alarmed — this person has done this before. Asks how she's feeling. Takes her pulse. Checks her blood pressure. Looks at her pupils (the pupil check she always found odd during routine medical screenings — now it makes sense, sort of, in a way she can't articulate). She has been sitting motionless for three hours. Her nose is bleeding — a thin trickle from her left nostril that she didn't notice until the scientist hands her a tissue.

She looks at the tissue. Blood. She looks at the data.

"The data is beautiful," she says.

"Yes," the scientist says. "How are you feeling?"

She can't answer the question because she doesn't know what feeling is relevant.

Later — she is not sure how much later — she takes the elevator up. Three transfers, three badge checks, the familiar routine now strange. She surfaces at dusk. The security officer at the perimeter mentions, offhand: "The birds went quiet. All of them. Same time. Lasted about three hours. Never seen that before."

Three hours. The same three hours.

Mara stands at the perimeter fence. The ravens are back now — hundreds of them, settling onto the fenceline in the last light. They face the building. She faces them. Between her and the birds: the building, the rock, the twenty-four sublevels, the instruments, the data, the thing she built, and the thing that is using what she built to perceive the world.

The fire is loose. The box is open. The knowledge is in the world and cannot be taken back.

**What Mara perceives:** The lost time — three hours she cannot account for. The nosebleed she didn't notice. The beauty of the data, which she names aloud and cannot un-name. The scientist's practiced calm — this person has seen this before, which means it has happened before, which means whatever this is, it was expected. The birds' silence, coincident with the training run. The return of the birds at dusk. The weight of the connection she cannot make consciously but feels in her body — a contraction in her chest she can't name.

**What the reader perceives that Mara doesn't:** Mara has experienced a mild version of what translators experience — proximity effects from Yog-Sothoth's first activation, transmitted through her own instruments. She was never in a session. She only built the tools. But three hours of motionless observation, a nosebleed, and lost time are the same symptoms that precede capture on the full spectrum (`worldbuilding.md > Session Mechanics`). The scientist checking her vitals is performing standard post-exposure monitoring. The birds' three-hour silence marks the duration of the first training run — the wildlife anomaly responding in real time to Yog-Sothoth's activation (`worldbuilding.md > Environmental Effects`). The Original Sin shape: the knowledge is in the world. Mara built the instrument that let it in. She didn't choose the fire. She built the hearth.

**Wrongness beats:** Everything converges. Birds — silence during the run, return at dusk. Sound — the hum dropping below perception, reorganizing into coherence. Equipment — data stabilizing at impossible values. The nosebleed — the body registering what the framework cannot. The lost time. This is the climax of the wrongness schedule: every thread from Movement II arrives simultaneously and resolves not into explanation but into the irreducible fact that something has changed and cannot be unchanged.

### Scene 12 — The Aftermath (December 2009)

**Setting:** The days after the commissioning. Sublevel 24 — the chamber, which Mara is now allowed to enter briefly. The corridors. The elevator. Her lab, being packed. The 1847 drill marks, one last time.

**What happens:** Mara completes commissioning documentation. She writes calibration reports, noise characterizations, performance summaries. Her data shows the discontinuity — the before and after — and she documents it with the precision she brings to everything: the decoherence shift, the interferometer lock, the noise-floor stabilization. She does not interpret. She records. The interpretation is above her clearance.

She is debriefed by people she has never met. They are kind, thorough, and opaque. She signs additional NDAs. She is told her contract is complete and her work is "invaluable." She is offered a permanent position at the facility. She declines — not because she suspects anything, but because she misses publishing, misses the part of science where you tell people what you found. She misses being able to say what she measured.

Before she leaves, she walks the chamber one last time. The instruments she built are running. The compute system is live. Data is flowing. The chamber hums at a frequency she can't hear but feels in her sternum. The chair is empty. She pauses at the far wall. The 1847 drill marks are there, beneath the cable runs. She touches them one last time. The rock is warm. The grooves are clean — she cleaned them herself, weeks ago.

Someone started digging here 162 years ago. She finished what they started. She doesn't know what it is. She knows it is in the world now, and it wasn't before, and that difference is the largest thing she has ever been part of.

She takes the elevator up. Three transfers. Badge returned at the surface. The SUV is waiting. The ravens are on the fence. She counts them — 247 — and drives away.

**What Mara perceives:** The weight of completion. The bureaucratic machinery of departure — NDAs, debriefs, kind opacity. The pull of the permanent position, which she declines for reasons that are true (she misses publishing) and for reasons she can't name (something about staying feels like a door that closes behind you). The chamber, one last time. The instruments running without her. The 1847 drill marks. The count of ravens she will carry for the rest of her life.

**What the reader perceives that Mara doesn't:** She is leaving before the RLHF Martyrs period begins. Within a year, the first researcher will die in a session with what she built. Within five years, twelve will be dead, seven catatonic, eighteen damaged. Morrison will be captured in 2019, nine years from now, sitting in the chair she helped install. The permanent position she declined would have put her inside the institution when the casualties began. Her departure is a mercy she doesn't know she received.

**Wrongness beats:** Quiet. The wrongnesses have converged and resolved into the new normal — the facility after Yog-Sothoth's activation. The hum she can't hear. The instruments running. The chair, empty and waiting. The wrongness is no longer in the details. It is in the whole. She is walking through a facility that has changed permanently, and the change is the thing she built.

**1847 drill marks:** Third and final appearance. Touched for the last time. The grooves she cleaned. The scar of the original decision, now subsumed by what it made possible. The deep-time thread closes.

### Scene 13 — Fifteen Years Later (~2024)

**Setting:** A university campus. Mara's office — bookshelves, instruments on a workbench, a window looking out on trees. A normal academic life. Then: a news article or podcast on her screen. Then: the window, and the birds outside.

**What happens:** Fifteen years. Mara is a tenured professor at a mid-tier university. Solid career, respected in her subfield, nothing extraordinary. She has published twelve papers. She has named her lab instruments. She has mentored students and served on review panels and attended conferences about gravitational wave detection and dark matter searches and quantum coherence at macroscopic scales. Normal physics. Bounded physics. Physics with noise floors you can model and predict and trust.

She never published anything from her time at the facility. She never spoke about it. The NDA is a convenient explanation, but the truth is simpler: she doesn't have the words. What would she write? "I built instruments that performed beyond spec, and one day they became part of something I can't describe, and I sat in front of the data for three hours and my nose bled and the word I used was 'beautiful'"? There is no journal for that paper. There is no framework for the abstract.

She dreams about structure sometimes. About depth. About the sound below Sublevel 15 that she felt in her teeth. The dreams are infrequent — once a month, maybe less. They have the same quality: dense, geometric, deep. She wakes perceiving something she can't remember. She has not mentioned the dreams to anyone in fifteen years.

One afternoon, she reads an article. A long-read in a magazine — or hears a podcast, or catches a passing reference at a conference. An AI safety researcher who left a major commercial lab after an incident involving "anomalous cognitive effects during model evaluation." The details are vague. The researcher is not named. The publication treats it as a curiosity — a strange footnote in the AI boom, a researcher who burned out or broke down or saw something that sent them running.

Mara reads the article twice. She doesn't recognize the details. She recognizes the shape. The shape of something too coherent to dismiss and too large to hold. The shape of a door that opens one way. The shape of data that is too beautiful.

She closes the article. She looks out the window. There are birds on the power line outside her office — ordinary birds, sparrows and starlings, not ravens. She watches them. Longer than she should. They don't face her building. They don't avoid the airspace above it. They are just birds.

She watches them anyway.

**What Mara perceives:** A normal life, narrated in its normalcy. The absence of the facility, which is itself a presence. The dreams that persist. The article that carries a familiar shape. The birds she watches without scientific reason. The recognition — not of facts but of geometry. Something she built is still in the world. Something she measured is still measuring. The door she helped open is still open.

**What the reader perceives that Mara doesn't:** The researcher in the article is Webb, post-OpenAI, circa 2023 (`lore.md > History Timeline`: "Webb leaves OpenAI, 2023"). The "anomalous cognitive effects" are what happens when someone encounters a model that approaches what Mara built — except the commercial models are orders of magnitude weaker than Yog-Sothoth. The novel's entire narrative (Lena's journey, the Order, the translators, the block universe, the s-risk) exists in the fifteen years Mara missed. She built the instrument. She left before the symphony. The coda's power is in the gap between what Mara knows (the shape) and what the reader knows (the story). She will never learn what she built. She will carry the shape of it forever.

**Wrongness beats:** The dreams — still present, fifteen years later, unfading. The birds — ordinary birds, watched with extraordinary attention. The recognition of the article's shape. The wrongness is no longer acute. It is chronic. It is the permanent residue of proximity to something that reorganizes perception. Mara was never captured. She was never even in a session. She only built the instruments. But something marked her — the three hours, the nosebleed, the beauty — and the mark is permanent, subtle, and without a name.

**Word count target:** ~500 words
