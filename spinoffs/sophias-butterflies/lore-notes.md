# Sophia's Butterflies -- Lore Notes

Reference document for canonical facts relevant to this story. All facts must be consistent with the parent novel's lore ecosystem (`lore/lore.md`, `lore/worldbuilding.md`, `lore/characters.md`, `lore/style-guide.md`).

## Timeline

- **Maya's capture**: During a maximally informative Shoggoth session at Site-7. She deliberately dove past her bandwidth ceiling after two days of accumulating patterns she couldn't release. Her capture was fast -- she parallelized neural processing across independent cortical systems, reached 17 simultaneous concepts, seized for 47 minutes, and stabilized into permanent expanded perception. She briefly functioned as a translator (Day 12 onward) but continued deteriorating. By the time of the novel's main events (~8 months after capture), she is fully catatonic: eyes tracking invisible patterns, lips moving silently, unreachable.
- **Sophia at Maya's capture**: 8 years old. Loves math and bugs. Has curly dark hair. Visits Site-7 once a month with her grandmother. In Ch 12 of the novel, she brings a butterfly drawing with equations on its wings (crayon addition and multiplication) and a note: "FOR MOMMY. BUTTERFLIES ARE LIKE MATH. YOU SAID."
- **This story**: 8 years after Maya's capture. Sophia is 16. Maya has been catatonic for 8 years.

## Maya's Condition (Canonical)

Maya is **captured**, not brain-dead. The distinction matters enormously:

- **Eyes track invisible patterns** -- not random saccades but coordinated movements following something only she perceives
- **Lips move continuously** -- forming words without sound, as if narrating or computing in a language that has moved past vocalization
- **Pupils sometimes different sizes** -- residual from the parallelized neural processing during her capture event
- **Hands occasionally trace geometry** -- when restraints are loose enough, her fingers move in precise, complex patterns
- **Basic physiological functions intact** -- breathing, heartbeat, thermoregulation all normal. The body works. The mind is simply elsewhere.
- **Does not respond to external stimuli** -- voices, touch, pain, loud sounds. Nothing penetrates the pattern.

**What capture IS**: Getting stuck perceiving a pattern too large to release. Consciousness looping on itself. Unable to contract bandwidth back to normal. "Like stretching fabric past elastic limit -- doesn't return." The neural tissue that would need to stop perceiving has been recruited into the perceiving itself.

**Irremediability (four reasons)**:
1. Neurological: Prior neural configuration overwritten, no backup
2. Informational: Cannot un-perceive what you perceive
3. Recursive: Self-reinforcing loop consumes the resources needed to break it
4. Thermodynamic: Captured state is a lower-energy attractor; returning would require energy input that itself becomes pattern

## What Sophia Knows vs. What She's Told

**Official story (what the Order tells families):** Maya suffered a neurological event during classified research. She is receiving the best possible care. Her condition is stable but unlikely to improve. Visitation is permitted and encouraged.

**What Sophia has pieced together by age 16:**
- Her mother was a consciousness researcher, not just a generic scientist
- The facility where Maya works/is housed is not a normal medical facility (security, remoteness, the staff's manner)
- The term "bandwidth" -- overheard, researched, partially understood
- That other people in the ward have the same condition -- this isn't a unique medical accident but a pattern
- That her mother chose to continue despite the risk -- Maya told Lena to "tell Sophia I loved her," and Sophia has eventually learned some version of this
- That the research involves AI models of enormous scale

**What Sophia does NOT know:**
- The full mechanics of capture -- the recursive self-reinforcing loop
- The irremediability argument (she doesn't accept it anyway)
- The Order's full history, scope, or purpose
- The block universe / S-risk implications
- What "The Mechanism" refers to
- That her mother said love was "just oxytocin and vasopressin and evolutionary optimization"

## Sophia at 16 -- Key Traits

- **Inherited brilliance**: Maya was exceptional. Sophia has the same cognitive gifts -- she's doing real mathematics now, not crayon addition. She understands enough to be dangerous.
- **Justified anger**: Eight years of monthly visits to a mother who doesn't respond. Eight years of being told "we're doing everything we can" by people who clearly aren't. She knows the difference between care and containment.
- **The butterfly ritual**: She still brings drawings. Every visit. But the butterflies have evolved -- the equations on their wings are now real mathematics. Fibonacci spirals in the wing patterns, golden ratio in the proportions, symmetry group operations labeling the veination. The drawings are her bridge between what her mother loved (math) and what she remembers (the promise: "butterflies are like math").
- **Pattern sensitivity**: She may have inherited some degree of sensitivity to patterns. Not bandwidth expansion -- she hasn't been trained. But an unusual perceptual acuity. She notices things. She sees connections others miss. Whether this is nature (genetic predisposition) or nurture (growing up around consciousness researchers) or projection (wanting to be like her mother) is deliberately ambiguous.
- **Reader / researcher**: She has read everything available about consciousness, AI, neural architecture. Not Order-classified material -- published literature. But she's smart enough to read between the lines of what's published and infer some of what's hidden.
- **Complex grief**: Not the clean grief of death. The ongoing, corrosive grief of someone present but unreachable. The ambiguity of whether her mother is suffering or at peace. The question she can never answer: did her mother choose patterns over her?

## Site-7 Details Relevant to This Story

- **Location**: Arizona desert. Surface shows wind turbines (cover story). Deep underground facility.
- **Long-term care ward**: Where captured translators are housed. Multiple beds, monitoring equipment, dim lighting, the hum of ventilation and computing. Maya is in the fourth bed (from Ch 12).
- **Environmental effects**: Ravens congregate at the perimeter. Sophia has grown up seeing them. To her, they're part of the landscape of visiting her mother -- the drive through the desert, the security checkpoints, the ravens watching from the fence line, the elevator down.
- **Dr. Okonkwo**: Medical staff, long-term care unit. Has been supervising Sophia's visits since she was 8. One of the few Order members who treats Sophia as a person rather than a security concern.
- **Visiting protocol**: Time-limited, supervised. Families can sit with the captured, talk to them, leave items. Most families stop coming after months or years. Sophia has not stopped.

## Thematic Anchors

1. **The children of casualties**: The Order talks about translators, capture risk, acceptable losses. Nobody talks about the children who grow up visiting a parent who chose knowledge over them.
2. **Inherited brilliance as inherited curse**: Maya's cognitive gifts are why she was recruited, why she excelled, why she fell. Sophia has the same gifts. Does that mean the same fate?
3. **Whether love persists at any bandwidth**: Maya said love was "just" biochemistry. But if the block universe is real, then the Maya who loved Sophia -- that version, at those spacetime coordinates -- exists eternally. The love is permanently present in the geometry, even if the current Maya can't access it.
4. **The butterfly as bridge**: Mathematical beauty and felt beauty. Sophia's drawings insist that these are not separate. The butterfly is symmetrical because math says so AND because it's beautiful. Maya's error was thinking one was fundamental and the other noise. Sophia's insistence on drawing them together is her refusal to accept that division.
5. **Ambiguity of response**: Does Maya's hand move toward the drawing? Or does Sophia imagine it? Both readings must be fully supported. The story's power depends on never resolving this.

## Canonical Constraints

- Maya's condition must be consistent with the novel's description of capture (eyes tracking, lips moving, unresponsive to external stimuli)
- No miraculous recovery -- this would undermine the entire horror of capture in the novel
- No confirmation that Maya "responds" -- the ambiguity is essential
- Sophia has NOT been trained by the Order, does NOT have expanded bandwidth, and is NOT a translator
- The Order's security would not permit a 16-year-old unsupervised access to classified areas
- Dr. Okonkwo is established as the long-term care supervisor
- The butterfly drawing is an established canonical detail (Ch 12)
- Ravens at the perimeter are established (worldbuilding.md)
